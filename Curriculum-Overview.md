### Core Learning Priorities & Diagnostic Challenges
1. **The Elementary Vocabulary Trap**: YH relies heavily on generic, low-impact verbs (*do, make, have, talk, say, think, get, fix, see, good, bad, big, problem*). He needs a systematic upgrade into **Tier-3 Executive Vernacular** (*execute, allocate, deliberate, convey, formulate, mitigate, critical bottleneck, optimal, feasible*).
2. **Katakana English & *Wasei-Eigo* Interference**: High vulnerability to Japanese-invented English words (*claim, cost-performance, appoint, remake, NG, consent, salaryman*) and adding trailing vowels to consonant codas (*pro-jec-to*, *clien-to*, *risu-ku*).
3. **Mora-Timed Hesitation vs. English Stress-Timing**: Japanese speech rhythm gives equal duration to every syllable/mora. When speaking English, this causes flat, monotonous delivery and rapid vocal fatigue. He needs stress-timing: punching tonic content words and reducing auxiliary syllables.
4. **Hesitancy & Apologetic Discourse (*Enryo / Aimai*)**: Cultural reluctance to be direct leads to prolonged filler stalls (*"Ano...", "Eto...", "I'm sorry, but..."*). He requires structured English discourse formulas like the **PREP Framework** (Point, Reason, Example, Point) and **Diplomatic Softening Hedges** to speak assertively yet gracefully.

## 2. Japanese-to-English Linguistic Transfer Matrix

Native Japanese speakers operating at an intermediate conversational level face distinct phonological, morphological, and sociolinguistic transfer patterns:

| Linguistic Feature | Japanese Native Transfer Pattern | Target International Business English Feature | Curriculum Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Speech Rhythm** | **Mora-timed**: Every syllable/mora receives equal acoustic duration and pitch weight. | **Stress-timed**: Dynamic cadence; stressed syllables hit regular musical pulses, unstressed words reduce to Schwa (/ə/). | **Week 1 & 2** |
| **Consonant Codas & Epenthesis** | **Vowel addition**: Adding /u/ or /o/ after final consonants (*risu-ku*, *clien-to*, *desu-ku*). | **Crisp consonant termination**: Clean stop closures without trailing vowel release (/rɪsk/, /ˈklaɪənt/). | **Week 1 & 3** |
| **Liquid Consonants (/l/ vs. /r/)** | Alveolar tap [ɾ] used for both /l/ and /r/, creating confusion in key business words (*lead/read*, *client*, *deliverable*). | Distinct lateral alveolar /l/ (tongue tip firmly on alveolar ridge) vs. post-alveolar approximant /r/ (curled tongue, lips rounded). | **Week 2 & 4** |
| **Katakana False Friends (*Wasei-Eigo*)** | Using loanwords with Japanese meanings (*claim* = complaint; *appoint* = meeting; *cost-performance* = ROI). | Precise professional business terminology (*lodge a complaint*, *calendar invite*, *cost-benefit / ROI*). | **Week 1 & 4** |
| **Sentence Structure & Verb Placement** | **SOV (Subject-Object-Verb)**: Verb placed at end; tends to delay core business action or result in English. | **SVO (Subject-Verb-Object)**: Front-loading action verbs and key takeaways immediately for executive clarity. | **Week 1 & 3** |
| **Discourse Fillers & Hesitation** | Defaulting to *"Ano..."*, *"Eto..."*, or awkward silence while mentally planning Japanese grammar. | **Executive Bridge Starters**: *"Looking at the current numbers..."*, *"From a strategic standpoint..."*, *"To build on that point..."*. | **Week 1 & 2** |
| **Business Politeness & Pushback** | Apologetic indirectness (*"I am sorry, but..."* or silence) to avoid conflict (*wa*). | **Diplomatic Assertiveness**: *"I see the merit in that; however, from an operational timeline perspective..."* | **Week 3 & 4** |
| **Word Choice (Lexicon)** | Repeating basic Tier-1 conversational vocabulary (*make better, have a talk, big trouble*). | **Tier-3 High-Impact Collocations & Phrasal Verbs**: (*streamline operations, touch base, iron out bottlenecks*). | **All Weeks** |

## 4. The 50-Minute Lesson Architecture

To maximize YH's active student speaking time (>70%) while aggressively expanding his business vocabulary and refining his pronunciation, each weekly session follows this 5-phase structure:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            50-MINUTE BUSINESS ENGLISH LESSON ARCHITECTURE                         |
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+---------------+
| Phase 1 (8-10m)    | Phase 2 (10-12m)   | Phase 3 (12-15m)   | Phase 4 (12-15m)   | Phase 5 (3-5m)|
| Executive Warm-Up  | Katakana           | 3-Tier Lexical     | Workplace          | Cool-Down &   |
| & Vocabulary       | Deconstruction &   | Upgrade (Business  | Simulation Lab     | Recast Matrix |
| Retrieval Sprint   | Phonetic Clinic    | Collocations)      | (Roleplay / PREP)  | (Action Plan) |
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+---------------+
```

### Phase 1: Executive Warm-Up & Vocabulary Retrieval Sprint (8–10 mins)
- **Objective**: Break cognitive inertia, eliminate Japanese filler stalls (*"Eto..."*), and force rapid vocabulary retrieval under time pressure.
- **Techniques**:
  - *Rapid-Fire Synonym Ladder*: Tutor says a basic Tier-1 verb (*"make"*); student has 3 seconds to fire off 3 Tier-2/3 upgrades (*"generate, formulate, fabricate"*).
  - *Katakana Purge Drill*: Convert Wasei-eigo scenarios into executive English within 5 seconds.
  - *The 30-Second Elevator Pitch*: Spontaneous answer to an unexpected workplace scenario using the PREP framework.

### Phase 2: Katakana Deconstruction & Phonetic Clinic (10–12 mins)
- **Objective**: Dismantle vowel epenthesis, master the 2-syllable noun/verb stress shift, and ensure crisp consonant closures.
- **Techniques**:
  - *Vowel Chopping*: Eliminating trailing /u/ and /o/ sounds (*risk*, *client*, *product*).
  - *Stress Shift Diagnostic*: Contrasting noun stress (1st syllable) vs. verb stress (2nd syllable): *PROJ-ect* vs. *pro-JECT*, *CON-duct* vs. *con-DUCT*, *REC-ord* vs. *re-CORD*.
  - *L vs. R Articulatory Precision*: Tongue placement drills on high-frequency business pairs (*lead/read, collaborate, realistic, deliverable*).

### Phase 3: 3-Tier Executive Lexical Upgrade (12–15 mins)
- **Objective**: Replace conversational crutch words with 5–7 high-frequency business collocations, executive idioms, and workplace phrasal verbs.
- **Components**:
  1. *The 3-Tier Ladder*: Contrast Elementary (Tier 1) ➔ Conversational (Tier 2) ➔ Executive (Tier 3).
  2. *Contextual Collocations*: Words that naturally live together in global business (*streamline operations, bridge the gap, mitigate risks, strike a compromise*).
  3. *Micro-Production*: YH immediately formulates 2 real-world sentences connecting each phrase to his actual work projects.

### Phase 4: Workplace Simulation Lab & Roleplay (12–15 mins)
- **Objective**: Stress-test target vocabulary and prosody in high-realism business simulations tailored to YH's 7 key domains.
- **Components**:
  - Dynamic Roleplay Cards (Tutor plays skeptical client, pushy colleague, or hiring manager; Student defends proposal).
  - Enforcing the **PREP Framework**:
    - **P**oint: Clear thesis statement.
    - **R**eason: Operational or financial rationale.
    - **E**xample: Concrete real-world case or data point.
    - **P**oint: Forward-looking summary / call to action.
  - Silent tracking by tutor: noting lexical misses and pronunciation slips without interrupting natural flow.

### Phase 5: Cool-Down, Recast Matrix & Action Plan (3–5 mins)
- **Objective**: Consolidate breakthroughs and deliver positive, actionable feedback.
- **Components**:
  - Review 3 vocabulary upgrades produced by YH during the roleplay.
  - Review 2 pronunciation refinements.
  - Assign weekly voice memo / portal homework assignment on the GitHub Pages suite.

## 5. 4-Week Core Curriculum Syllabus

```
Week 1: Executive Meeting Agility & The High-Impact Vocabulary Upgrade
- Domain Focus: Meetings & Teamwork (🤝, 💼).
- Phonetics & Prosody: Cutting vowel epenthesis (/u/ and /o/ chopping); The 2-syllable noun/verb stress shift; Rhythm isochrony.
- Lexical Focus: The 3-Tier Verb Ladder (execute, streamline, delegate, mitigate, evaluate); Wasei-Eigo purge (claim -> complaint, appo -> sync, cospa -> ROI).
- Business Framework: The PREP Model for spontaneous meeting contributions; Diplomatic agreement & soft disagreement.
- Simulation Lab: Cross-functional sprint sync: unblocking a critical bottleneck and realigning project deliverables.

Week 2: Product Presentations, Feature Demos & Data Narration
- Domain Focus: Presentations & Communicating with Clients (🗣️, 📞).
- Phonetics & Prosody: Liquid consonants /l/ vs. /r/ in executive speech (deliverable, client, reliable, realistic); Tonic pitch jumps for emphasis.
- Lexical Focus: Presentation signposting & transitions (walk you through, drill down into, shed light on, bring to the table); Describing trends and metrics (exponential, plateau, dip, outpace).
- Business Framework: The "Hook, Solution, Proof, CTA" pitch structure.
- Simulation Lab: Delivering a 3-minute client product pitch and handling tough Q&A on pricing and implementation timeline.

Week 3: Cross-Functional Team Leadership & Constructive Feedback
- Domain Focus: Managing Teams & Teamwork (👥, 💼).
- Phonetics & Prosody: Intonation of diplomatic authority; Modulating pace and pausing (The 4 Ps: Power, Pause, Pace, Pitch) to sound grounded.
- Lexical Focus: Delegation and alignment verbs (allocate, empower, oversee, steer, benchmark); Constructive feedback collocations (room for improvement, align on expectations, constructive takeaways).
- Business Framework: The SBI Feedback Framework (Situation, Behavior, Impact) + Actionable Next Steps.
- Simulation Lab: Conducting a 1-on-1 performance review with an underperforming team member or misaligned colleague.

Week 4: High-Stakes Negotiations & Client Boundary Setting
- Domain Focus: Negotiations & Client Communication (📊, 📞).
- Phonetics & Prosody: Fall-Rise intonation for tactful reservation; Eliminating apologetic starts ("I'm sorry but...").
- Lexical Focus: Negotiation idioms and phrasal verbs (hammer out, hold ground, give and take, carve out, sweeten the deal, non-negotiable); Framing trade-offs (contingent upon, mutually beneficial).
- Business Framework: Conditional concession framing: *"If you can meet us on X, we would be in a position to concede Y."*
- Simulation Lab: High-stakes contract renegotiation: client requests scope creep and budget discount; student holds ground diplomatically.
```

## 6. Tutor In-Class Execution Guidelines (For Lewis)

### How to Accelerate YH's Vocabulary Retrieval
1. **Ban the Basic Crutch Words**: Challenge YH in advance: *"In today's simulation, words like 'make', 'do', 'good', and 'problem' are off limits. Let's see how many Tier-3 upgrades you can drop into the conversation!"*
2. **The "Upgrade Ping-Pong" Drill**: When YH defaults to a Tier-1 word during casual talk (e.g., *"We had a big problem with the client"*), do not stop his train of thought. Immediately hold up two fingers or type in the chat box: `critical bottleneck` or `major escalation`. Signal him to repeat that clause with the new phrase.
3. **The 3-Second Cognitive Rule**: If YH pauses looking at the ceiling, looking for a Japanese word, give him an executive connector: *"Start with: 'From an operational standpoint...' Go!"*
4. **Physicalizing the Metronome**: Tap your knuckles on the desk at 60 BPM to establish English stress-timed rhythm. Encourage YH to punch the stressed syllables with his hand so he physically experiences the cadence.
5. **The Recast Protocol**:
   - Incorrect: *"We got a big claim from the client about the cost-performance."*
   - Tutor Recast: *"Understood—so the client **lodged a formal complaint** regarding the **ROI** of the product. How did the team respond?"*
   - Outcome: YH hears the native phrase immediately reinforced in context without experiencing performance anxiety.
