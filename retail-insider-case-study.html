Retail Insider Case Study · Food Retail & Cross-Border Expansion

From Convenience to Culinary Destination: How 7-Eleven Canada Turned a Japanese Staple into a Growth Engine

Facing fierce competition from discount grocers and third-party delivery apps, 7-Eleven Canada—operating roughly 550 stores across Ontario and British Columbia—is executing a bold pivot. Under Vice President and General Manager Marc Goodman, the chain is shifting away from traditional convenience-store reliance toward a "restaurant-first" model anchored in fresh prepared food and proprietary beverages.

The breakout catalyst for this strategy came from Japan. In early 2026, 7-Eleven Canada imported an iconic Japanese convenience-store staple: the tamago sando, a Japanese-style egg salad sandwich made with soft shokupan milk bread, cage-free eggs, and Kewpie mayonnaise. The item went viral on social media and rapidly became the chain's #1 best-selling sandwich, proving that authentic international concepts can command premium demand in overseas markets.

However, scaling fresh, perishable food requires far more than a viral recipe. To protect quality and margins during the rollout, 7-Eleven Canada backed its stores with four dedicated commissaries to guarantee daily consistency. At the same time, the company began testing licensed sit-down dining formats—serving hot meals alongside chilled beer and wine—while expanding franchising to accelerate its regional footprint.

Encouraged by the egg salad sandwich breakthrough, 7-Eleven Canada is now importing top-selling Korean snacks and partnering with local specialty producers. Rather than acting as a passive reseller, the retailer has turned its physical stores into an agile, cross-border culinary test bed—a playbook with clear lessons for any food brand preparing to scale internationally.
