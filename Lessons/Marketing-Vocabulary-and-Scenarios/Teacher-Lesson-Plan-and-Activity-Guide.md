## 🎯 Pedagogical Overview & How to Use This Suite

This module adapts **11 core activities** from Jackie Bolen’s *101 Activities and Resources for Teaching English Online* specifically for **YH’s primary goal**: **expanding his active business & marketing vocabulary** and **executing realistic marketing, commercial, and stakeholder scenarios** (without spending class time on accent or pronunciation drills).

### Core Coaching Rules for All 11 Activities:
1. **Ban Tier-1 Crutch Words**: During these scenarios, words like *make, get, good, bad, big problem, change, buy,* and *cheap* are off-limits. Whenever YH uses one, hold up a finger or type the Tier-3 marketing equivalent in the chat so he immediately recasts the sentence.
2. **Enforce Business Nuance Over Dictionary Definitions**: Do not let YH stop to look up words in a Japanese dictionary. Use context clues, trade-off comparisons, and commercial cause-and-effect.
3. **Track Live Usage with the Word Bank**: In every simulation, display the 6–8 target marketing collocations on screen. YH only "clears" the scenario when he has naturally deployed at least 5 of the target terms in context.

## Activity 1: Live Ad Copy & Landing Page Tear-Down
* **Adapted From**: *Flyer Time* (pp. 127–128) & *Brochure Scanning* (pp. 112–114)
* **Time**: 15 Minutes | **Skills**: Reading for Detail, Strategic Inference, Marketing Vocabulary
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: Instead of scanning tourist brochures or festival flyers, YH audits two realistic B2B/B2C promotional assets—identifying explicit facts and inferring strategic flaws in positioning, target persona, and CTA design.

### Step-by-Step Procedure:
1. Share **Mockup A** and **Mockup B** (below or in `Student-Handout-and-Scenario-Pack.md`) on screen.
2. Give YH **90 seconds** to scan both without a dictionary.
3. Ask the **Explicit & Inferential Audit Questions** below. Require YH to use at least **4 words from the Target Word Bank** in his critique.

### Target Word Bank for Activity 1:
`value proposition` · `target persona` · `pain point` · `call-to-action (CTA)` · `friction` · `social proof` · `overhaul` · `conversion rate`

### The Two Campaign Mockups to Audit:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| MOCKUP A: "CloudSync Pro — The Best File Software for Everyone!"                  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Headline:   We Made a Great New Cloud Tool With 256-Bit Encryption & 40 Features! |
| Subhead:    Our engineers worked hard for 2 years to build this system for you.   |
| Body Copy:  Do you want to put files on the computer? CloudSync Pro has folders,  |
|             color tags, admin settings, API webhooks, and family photo sharing.   |
|             Pricing starts at $9/month, $29/month, $49/month, $89/month, or       |
|             custom enterprise pricing.                                            |
| Button/CTA: [ Click Here to Submit an Inquiry Form & Wait for a Sales Call ]      |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| MOCKUP B: "FlowDesk — Eliminate 12 Hours of Manual Client Reporting Every Week"   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Headline:   Stop Copy-Pasting Metrics Across 6 Spreadsheets Every Friday.         |
| Subhead:    Built for Digital Marketing Agencies managing 10+ client accounts.    |
| Body Copy:  Automatically pull Google Ads, Meta, and CRM data into white-labeled  |
|             client dashboards in 60 seconds. Trusted by 1,400+ agency directors   |
|             to cut reporting overhead by 80% and boost client retention.          |
| Button/CTA: [ Start Your 14-Day Free Trial — No Credit Card Required ]            |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Teacher Audit Questions & Model Answers:
1. **Explicit Question**: *"Looking at Mockup B, who is the exact **target persona**, and what quantified outcome does the **value proposition** promise?"*
   - *Model Answer*: It targets digital marketing agency directors managing 10+ accounts, promising to eliminate 12 hours of manual reporting per week and cut reporting overhead by 80%.
2. **Inferential Question**: *"Why will Mockup A suffer from a terrible **conversion rate**? Identify 3 strategic flaws in its messaging, pricing, and **CTA**."*
   - *Model Answer*:
     1. **Muddy Target Persona**: It tries to sell to both B2B IT admins ("API webhooks") and B2C families ("family photo sharing"), so it resonates with no one.
     2. **Feature-Dumping vs. Pain Point**: The headline talks about the company's ego ("our engineers worked for 2 years") and technical features instead of solving a customer **pain point**.
     3. **High-Friction CTA & Decision Paralysis**: Having 5 pricing tiers confuses buyers, and the CTA ("Submit a form and wait for a sales call") creates massive **friction** compared to Mockup B's frictionless 14-day trial.
3. **Actionable Upgrade Task**: *"You are the Marketing Director. Tell the copywriter in 3 sentences how to **overhaul** Mockup A."*

## Activity 2: Marketing Channel & Agency Selection Matrix (\$50,000 Q3 Budget)
* **Adapted From**: *Real House Hunters* (p. 60)
* **Time**: 15 Minutes | **Skills**: Evaluating Trade-Offs, Comparative Analysis, Budget Pitching
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: In *Real House Hunters*, students evaluate 3 houses across budget, location, and repairs to defend a choice. Here, YH evaluates **3 Q3 Marketing Channel Proposals** with a **\$50,000 budget**, comparing CAC, ROAS, speed, and scalability before pitching his allocation to the CMO.

### Target Word Bank for Activity 2:
`allocate budget` · `Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)` · `Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)` · `trade-off` · `lead generation` · `brand awareness` · `attribution` · `cost-effective`

### The 3 Marketing Channel Proposals:

| Criteria | Option A: Paid Search & LinkedIn Ads (SEM) | Option B: Industry KOL & YouTube Sponsorships | Option C: B2B Executive Webinar & Whitepaper Funnel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upfront Cost** | \$50,000 (\$40k ad spend + \$10k agency fee) | \$45,000 (3 tier-1 tech creators) | \$30,000 (Production, promotion + CRM automation) |
| **Time to Launch** | Immediate (Live in 5 days) | 4 weeks (Contracting & video production) | 6 weeks (Content creation & partner co-marketing) |
| **Projected Outcome** | 250 high-intent demo requests; **CAC: \$200** | 180,000 views; strong **brand awareness**, but direct **attribution** is murky | 600 qualified email leads + **evergreen nurture sequence**; **CAC: \$50** |
| **The Catch (Risk)** | Ad costs stop the second you stop paying; CPC is rising 15% Q-o-Q. | Creators control the script; hard to track exact **ROAS**. | Slowest initial ramp-up; requires internal team **bandwidth** to host. |

### Teacher Facilitation Procedure:
1. **Step 1 (Channel Breakdown — 5 mins)**: Ask YH to walk through Options A, B, and C, summarizing the **upside** and **downside / trade-off** of each channel in his own words.
2. **Step 2 (The CMO Challenge — 5 mins)**: Tell YH: *"Our CEO wants fast pipeline growth this quarter, but our CFO is furious that our CAC was too high last quarter. You have \$50,000. Do you put 100% into one option, or split the budget? Defend your decision."*
3. **Step 3 (Teacher Pushback — 5 mins)**: Challenge whichever option YH chooses (e.g., if he chooses Option C, say: *"Six weeks is too slow! Sales needs leads this month!"*) so he has to propose a hybrid allocation (e.g., allocating \$20k to Paid Search for immediate traction while investing \$30k into the Webinar funnel to lower long-term CAC).

## Activity 3: Campaign Analytics & Funnel Narration (60-Second Sprint)
* **Adapted From**: *Test Prep: Describing a Photo* (pp. 69–71) & *Picture Prompt* (pp. 232–235)
* **Time**: 12–15 Minutes | **Skills**: Data Storytelling, Speculating on Root Causes, Timed Fluency
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: Instead of describing a casual photo of people on a beach, YH is given a **Q2 Marketing Funnel & A/B Test Dashboard** and a **60-second timer**. He must describe what is happening in the funnel, diagnose where the leak is, and propose an optimization.

### Target Word Bank for Activity 3:
`impressions` · `click-through rate (CTR)` · `bounce rate` · `conversion funnel` · `critical bottleneck` · `spike` · `plateau` · `split test (A/B test)`

### Visual Data Prompt (Share on Screen):
```
===================================================================================
          Q2 CAMPAIGN PERFORMANCE DASHBOARD (APRIL vs. MAY)
===================================================================================
Stage 1: Ad Impressions         April: 120,000      May: 310,000   (+158% Spike)
Stage 2: Ad Clicks (CTR)        April: 2.5% (3,000) May: 3.2% (9,920) (+230% Growth)
Stage 3: Landing Page Bounce    April: 38%          May: 79%       (CRITICAL LEAK!)
Stage 4: Free Trial Signups     April: 240          May: 245       (Plateaued)

Note: In May, we launched a bold new social ad promising "Instant AI Automation in
```

### Teacher Facilitation Procedure:
1. **Pre-Teach Speculative & Analytical Starters (2 mins)** *(adapted from p. 70 & p. 233)*:

2. **Run the 60-Second Timer (5 mins)**: Start a visible 60-second stopwatch. YH must narrate all 4 stages of the dashboard, explain the root cause of the Stage 3 drop-off, and state his action plan before the timer hits `00:00`.
3. **Follow-Up Q&A (5 mins)**: Ask: *"Why did Free Trial Signups **plateau** at 245 even though Ad Clicks more than tripled? What two **A/B tests** should we run on Monday morning?"*

## Activity 4: Product Repositioning & New Market Entry Brainstorm
* **Adapted From**: *What Can I Do With a _____?* (p. 79) & *5 Senses* (pp. 161–162)
* **Time**: 10–12 Minutes | **Skills**: Creative Commercial Brainstorming, Benefits vs. Features
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: In the book, the teacher holds up a mundane object (like a potato or office item) and brainstorms creative uses. Here, we take a **stagnating product or feature** and brainstorm **3 new B2B/B2C target segments or use cases** to reposition it for higher margins.

### Target Word Bank for Activity 4:
`reposition` · `untapped market segment` · `use case` · `product-market fit` · `differentiate` · `upsell` · `bundle` · `value driver`

### Choose 1 of These 3 Product Cases:
1. **Case 1 (Hardware/IoT)**: Your company makes a high-accuracy noise-canceling directional microphone originally sold to **podcasters and gamers**, but the gaming market is saturated and margins are shrinking.
   - *Brainstorm Prompt*: Brainstorm **3 B2B commercial use cases** (e.g., *call center reps in noisy open offices, telemedicine doctors, drive-thru order kiosks, factory floor voice commands*) and pitch how you would **reposition** the marketing message for the highest-paying segment.
2. **Case 2 (SaaS/App)**: Your company built a simple **language flashcard & voice-recording app** originally for individual students paying \$5/month, but individual **churn** is high.
   - *Brainstorm Prompt*: How can you **pivot** and **reposition** this exact platform into a high-ticket B2B enterprise solution (\$5,000/year)? Who are the 3 best corporate buyers (e.g., *airline cabin crew training, hotel hospitality chains, offshore customer support teams, multinational sales onboarding*)?
3. **Case 3 (Consumer/Retail)**: A premium Japanese matcha beverage brand is struggling to sell \$6 cans in Singapore supermarkets because shoppers view it as an expensive soda.
   - *Brainstorm Prompt*: How do you **reposition** the product away from "soft drinks" (e.g., *corporate wellness office subscriptions, pre-workout clean energy for boutique gyms, luxury hotel minibar bundles*)?

## Activity 5: "Campaign in Crisis" Turnaround & Stakeholder Pushback
* **Adapted From**: *Agony Aunt* (pp. 108–109) & *Test Prep: Speaking to Essay Writing* (pp. 238–240)
* **Time**: 15 Minutes | **Skills**: Problem Diagnosis, Diplomatic Pushback, Defending Brand Equity
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: Combines *Agony Aunt* (reading a problem letter and prescribing advice) with *Speaking to Essay Writing* (p. 239: using linking phrases to address an opposing viewpoint before defending your own position with 3 concrete reasons).

### Target Word Bank for Activity 5:
`erode brand equity` · `cannibalize sales` · `short-term fix` · `messaging misalignment` · `nurture sequence` · `social proof` · `sweeten the deal` · `value-add`

### Opposing-Viewpoint Starters (Adapted from p. 239):
- *"Some on the sales team would argue that [slashing prices by 30%] is the fastest way to close deals; however, from a long-term brand equity standpoint..."*
- *"While I see the merit in [chasing viral traffic], the data shows that..."*
- *"I’m convinced that instead of discounting, our best move is to..."*

### The Crisis Brief ("Letter from the Sales Director"):
> **From**: Kenji Sato, Regional Sales Director  
> **Subject**: Urgent — We Need to Cut Prices by 30% Immediately!  
> *"Team, our Q3 enterprise software launch is in trouble. Marketing generated 500 leads from the recent 'Win a Free iPad' social media giveaway, but my sales reps say 90% of those leads refuse to buy when they hear our \$10,000 annual price tag. They say we are too expensive. I want Marketing to announce a 30% discount across the board this week so we can hit our Q3 quota!"*

### Teacher Facilitation Procedure:
1. Have YH read Kenji’s email and take 3 bullet notes on **what actually went wrong** (Hint: the "Win a Free iPad" giveaway attracted freebie-hunters, not qualified B2B decision-makers—a classic **lead-quality / targeting misalignment**, not a pricing problem!).
2. Have YH deliver a 90-second response to Kenji that:
   - Uses an **Opposing-Viewpoint Starter** to diplomatically reject the 30% price cut (explaining how discounting will **erode brand equity** and **cannibalize** existing renewals),
   - Diagnoses why the giveaway campaign generated low-intent leads, and
   - Proposes **2 value-add alternatives** to **sweeten the deal** without cutting the price (e.g., *a complimentary onboarding package, a 30-day pilot with ROI guarantee, or case-study social proof*).

## Activity 6: The 120-90-60 Go-To-Market (GTM) Elevator Pitch
* **Adapted From**: *120-90-60* (pp. 42–44)
* **Time**: 10–12 Minutes | **Skills**: Oral Fluency, Concise Synthesis, High-Density Vocabulary
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: One of the highest-impact speaking activities in Bolen's book. YH prepares 1–2 word bullet notes on a campaign or project he knows well, speaks continuously for **120 seconds**, then repeats **100% of the same core information** in **90 seconds**, and finally in **60 seconds**.
* **Why It Forces Vocabulary Upgrades**: You cannot fit a 2-minute explanation into 60 seconds just by talking faster—you *must* replace 10-word Tier-1 explanations (*"the money we spend to get one new customer to buy"*) with 1-word Tier-3 terms (*"CAC"*).

### Procedure:
1. **Prompt**: *"Pitch a new marketing initiative, product launch, or workflow improvement for your actual company (or one of today's case studies). Cover: (1) The market pain point, (2) The target persona, (3) The GTM channels, and (4) The expected ROI."*
2. **Prep (90s)**: YH writes **only 1–2 words per bullet** (no full sentences!).
3. **Round 1 (120 Seconds)**: YH delivers the full pitch while watching the countdown clock.
4. **30-Second Compression Pause**: Point out 2–3 places where YH used wordy phrasing and give him the Tier-3 shortcut (*spearhead, streamline, high-ROI, low-hanging fruit, outpace*).
5. **Round 2 (90 Seconds)** & **Round 3 (60 Seconds)**: YH delivers the exact same points in 90s, then 60s.

## Activity 7: Plain-English to Executive Marketing Upgrade
* **Adapted From**: *Sentence Substitution Ladder* (pp. 218–220) & *Paraphrasing Practice* (pp. 133–135)
* **Time**: 10 Minutes | **Skills**: Lexical Precision, Register Elevation

### Drill 7A: The 3-Step Sentence Substitution Ladder (p. 218)
*Rule: Change one bolded basic chunk at a time until the entire sentence is transformed into executive marketing English.*

1. **Base Sentence**: *"We want to **get more customers** by **changing** our **website button**."*
   - *Rung 1*: *"We want to **drive lead generation** by changing our website button."*
   - *Rung 2*: *"We want to drive lead generation by **overhauling** our website button."*
   - *Rung 3 (Executive)*: *"We aim to **drive lead generation** by **overhauling** our landing page **call-to-action (CTA)**."*

2. **Base Sentence**: *"This ad **costs too much money** and is **not making a big difference** in **sales**."*
   - *Rung 1*: *"This campaign has an **unsustainable CAC** and is not making a big difference in sales."*
   - *Rung 2*: *"This campaign has an unsustainable CAC and is **failing to move the needle** in sales."*
   - *Rung 3 (Executive)*: *"This campaign has an **unsustainable CAC** and is **failing to move the needle** on **quarterly revenue**."*

3. **Base Sentence**: *"If we **make the price cheaper**, we will **hurt our brand image** and **lose money**."*
   - *Rung 1*: *"If we **slash our pricing**, we will hurt our brand image and lose money."*
   - *Rung 2*: *"If we slash our pricing, we will **erode our brand equity** and lose money."*
   - *Rung 3 (Executive)*: *"If we **slash our pricing**, we will **erode our brand equity** and **compress our profit margins**."*

### Drill 7B: Paraphrasing a Marketing Brief (p. 133)
*Rule: Keep the immutable numbers/dates, combine clauses, and replace all underlined/basic words with Tier-3 marketing vocabulary.*

* **Original Basic Draft**:
  > *"In Q2, we spent \$20,000 on Instagram ads, which got 50,000 views, but almost nobody bought our product. We had a big problem because our website was too slow and people left right away. In Q3, we want to put that money into email marketing to keep our current customers happy."*
* **Model Executive Paraphrase**:
  > *"In Q2, we **allocated** \$20,000 to Instagram campaigns, generating 50,000 **impressions** but minimal **conversions**. Our **critical bottleneck** was page latency, which triggered a spike in **bounce rates**. For Q3, we propose **reallocating** that budget into automated **nurture sequences** to boost **client retention**."*

## Activity 8: Marketing Idioms "Closest in Meaning"
* **Adapted From**: *Closest in Meaning* (pp. 119–120)
* **Time**: 8 Minutes | **Skills**: Inferring Idiomatic Nuance from Context Clues (No Dictionary!)

*Rule: Read the focus sentence and choose which option (A, B, or C) is closest in meaning using context clues.*

1. **Focus**: *"Before we invest six months building a custom TikTok campaign, let's capture the **low-hanging fruit** by fixing our broken checkout page."*
   - A) Projects that are prestigious and win marketing awards.
   - B) Quick, easily achievable wins that deliver immediate results with minimal effort. ✅
   - C) Customers who only buy discounted products.

2. **Focus**: *"Launching a \$10 basic plan might attract freelancers, but we risk **cannibalizing** our \$50/month Pro tier."*
   - A) Taking revenue away from our own higher-priced product line. ✅
   - B) Aggressively attacking a rival company's market share.
   - C) Doubling our total advertising expenditure.

3. **Focus**: *"Our free ROI calculator acts as a **loss leader**—we don't make money on it directly, but 18% of users upgrade to our paid consulting package."*
   - A) A failed product that lost money due to poor management.
   - B) A salesperson who frequently misses their quarterly quota.
   - C) An offering priced below cost or free specifically to attract buyers into the paid ecosystem. ✅

4. **Focus**: *"After three months of slow sales in Singapore, our B2B referral program is finally **gaining traction**."*
   - A) Experiencing delays and technical bottlenecks.
   - B) Showing measurable momentum, adoption, and commercial results. ✅
   - C) Running out of allocated quarterly budget.

5. **Focus**: *"Changing the logo color is a nice cosmetic tweak, but it won't **move the needle** on our Q4 customer retention."*
   - A) Generate a noticeable, measurable impact on core metrics. ✅
   - B) Require approval from the legal department.
   - C) Reduce the workload of the design team.

## Activity 9: Business & Marketing Headline Prediction Lab (5Ws + H)
* **Adapted From**: *Headline Prediction Practice* (pp. 129–130)
* **Time**: 10 Minutes | **Skills**: Strategic Prediction, Executive Discussion, Skimming to Verify
* **Procedure**: Show YH the headline only. Have him predict **2–3 commercial reasons/hypotheses** using the *5Ws + H*, write them on the board, and then reveal the short case-study summary to compare.

### Headline 1:
> **"Why B2B SaaS Company 'MetricFlow' Deleted Its Free Plan—And Saw Paid Conversions Jump 42%"**
* **Teacher Prompt**: *"Why would deleting a free plan actually increase paid sales? Give me 2 marketing hypotheses."*
* **Case Summary Reveal**: MetricFlow discovered that 95% of its customer support **bandwidth** was being consumed by free-tier users who never upgraded. Worse, the free plan had limited features, giving prospects a poor impression of the product's **value proposition**. By replacing the free plan with a **14-day full-featured trial + guided onboarding**, they eliminated low-intent freebie seekers, cut support overhead by 60%, and boosted paid **conversion rates** by 42%.

### Headline 2:
> **"How a Tokyo Coffee Chain Cut Ad Spend to \$0 and Doubled Customer Retention in 6 Months"**
* **Teacher Prompt**: *"How do you double retention without spending a dollar on ads? What channels or mechanics did they use?"*
* **Case Summary Reveal**: Instead of spending \$30k/month on social media ads with high **CAC** and poor **attribution**, the chain **reallocated** its budget into an app-based **referral and loyalty subscription** (a "Loss Leader" morning coffee pass). Existing customers brought in colleagues (zero CAC), and daily app check-ins increased **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)** by 110%.

* **Adapted From**: *New Language Pile Up* (pp. 230–231) & *Write an Email* (pp. 199–200)
* **Time**: 5 Minutes at End of Class + Asynchronous Homework
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: Bolen recommends recording every new/recast term from a 1-on-1 lesson onto a dedicated pile of cards (p. 230), reviewing them in the final 5 minutes, and having the student write a brief, direct workplace email with a sharp subject line (pp. 199–200) using those exact terms for homework.

### End-of-Lesson & Homework Protocol:
1. **In the Final 5 Minutes**: List the **5–8 new marketing collocations** YH learned or had recast today.
2. **Homework Prompt (Write an Email — pp. 199–200)**:
   - **Rule 1 (Subject Line)**: No vague subject lines like *"Hello / Marketing update"*. Use an executive subject line: `[Action Required] Q3 Campaign Reallocation & CAC Optimization Plan`.
   - **Rule 2 (Brevity & Directness)**: Keep it under 150 words.
   - **Rule 3 (Lexical Integration)**: Write a follow-up email to your Marketing Director summarizing which Q2 campaign underperformed, why it happened, and how you propose reallocating the Q3 budget—using at least **6 terms from today's New Language Pile Up**.
