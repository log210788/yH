Welcome to Preply! As a new tutor on the platform, your greatest asset is not just your English fluency—it is your ability to create a **high-energy, psychologically safe, and structurally rigorous laboratory** where your student transforms from a hesitating speaker into an authoritative business executive.

Adult corporate students (like YH) are not taking English lessons to pass grammar tests. They have a single urgent metric: **Success at work (👔)**. They need to win deals, command boardrooms, pitch clients, and defend their ideas without feeling embarrassed or overlooked.

### The Golden Rule of 1-on-1 Preply Coaching
```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                      THE 70 / 30 TALKING RATIO                                     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  STUDENT TALKING TIME (STT): 70%                     |  TEACHER TALKING TIME (TTT): 30%            |
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Rookie Teacher Trap**: New tutors often fear silence. When a student pauses to think, the teacher jumps in, explains for 3 minutes, lectures on grammar, and ends with *"Does that make sense?"* The student nods politely (*"Yes"*), but has spoken for only 10 minutes of the 50-minute hour. 
> **Your Counter-Strategy**: Keep your explanations under 45 seconds. Put the framework on screen, model it once, and immediately demand production: *"Your turn—hit me with your opener. Go!"*

## 2. Platform Mechanics: Maximizing the Preply Virtual Classroom

### A. The 50-Minute Pacing Architecture
Never let a Preply lesson wander. Follow this strict 5-phase blueprint:

| Phase | Time | Name | What the Teacher Does | What the Student Does |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | 00:00 – 08:00 | **Executive Warm-Up & Anti-Translation Sprint** | Calls triggers at 3-second intervals; runs metronome / cadence drills. | Speaks spontaneously within 3 seconds; defines concepts; shakes off workday inertia. |
| **2** | 08:00 – 20:00 | **Core Pitching Framework & Delivery Clinic** | Introduces 1 key pitching concept (e.g., Frame Control, Hook, Data Arc) + webcam vocal mechanic. | Learns structural pattern; practices downward vocal inflection, stress shifts, and consonant stops. |
| **3** | 20:00 – 32:00 | **3-Tier Lexical Upgrade & Sentence Construction** | Displays Tier 1 ➔ Tier 3 vocabulary ladder; probes real-work relevance. | Replaces crutch words (*"make, fix, good"*) with power collocations; produces 2 work-relevant sentences. |
| **4** | 32:00 – 45:00 | **Live Workplace Simulation Lab (Roleplay)** | Plays skeptical client/investor; pushes back with realistic objections. | Delivers full 60–90 second pitch; defends proposal using target formulas. |
| **5** | 45:00 – 50:00 | **The Recast Matrix & Action Plan** | Delivers 3:2 feedback (3 wins, 2 high-yield recasts); briefs homework. | Notes down priority corrections; commits to weekly voice recording task. |

### B. Screen-Share Management: The "Eye Contact Switch"
A subtle mistake new tutors make is leaving their screen shared for all 50 minutes. When your screen is shared, the student looks at slides—not at a human face.

1. **Phases 1, 2, and 3 (Screen Share ON)**: Share your screen to display the warm-up matrix, vocabulary ladder, and structural templates.
2. **Phase 4 — The Simulation Lab (Screen Share OFF!)**:
   - Announce: *"Screen share off. We are now in a high-stakes client pitch. Look directly into your webcam lens—not at your notes, not at the floor. In 3... 2... 1... Pitch me!"*
   - This single technique forces the student to simulate real executive presence and prevents them from reading verbatim from a script.

---

### C. The Preply Chat Box: Your Live Subtitles Feed
Do not interrupt a student in the middle of a sentence to correct minor grammar slips. Interrupting shatters confidence and breaks narrative momentum.

Instead, use the Preply chat window as a **silent live subtitles feed**:
- While the student speaks, type their phrasing and the executive upgrade:
  - `❌ "Our product can do many things for your company."`
  - `💎 "Our solution drives operational efficiency across three core pillars."`
- In Phase 5 (or immediately after the drill), point to the chat: *"Look at line 2 in the chat. Read the diamond upgrade aloud with confidence!"*

## 3. Neuro-Linguistic & Cultural Diagnosis: Inside the Japanese Mind

Teaching a Japanese professional (like YH) requires deep empathy for the cultural and linguistic software running in their brain.

### A. The "Politeness Trap" (*Enryo*, *Aimai*, and *Okyaku-sama*)
1. **The Subservient Frame (*Okyaku-sama wa kamisama*)**:
   - In Japan, the cultural proverb is *"The customer is god."* Japanese business professionals are trained from day one to treat clients and partners with profound deference, humble verb forms (*kenjougo*), and continuous apologies (*sumimasen*).
   - **The International Consequence**: In global business, if you pitch a Western or international client like a subservient servant, **you lose immediately**. International decision-makers view extreme deference as a sign of weakness, low status, or lack of conviction. They buy from **experts and trusted peers**, not beggars.
   - **Your Coaching Script**:
     > *"YH, in international business, confidence is a form of politeness. When you apologize or hesitate, you waste the buyer's time and make them nervous. When you speak with calm authority, you show respect for their time and their intelligence. We are going to elevate you from a subservient vendor to a trusted executive partner."*

2. **The Ambiguity Shield (*Aimai*)**:
   - Japanese speakers often use non-committal words (*"maybe", "perhaps", "I think possibly"*) to avoid conflict.
   - **Your Fix**: Ban the word *"maybe"* during pitches. Teach them definitive modal verbs: *"We will", "Our data demonstrates", "This enables you to"*.

3. **Apologetic Fillers (*"I'm sorry, but..."*)**:
   - If a student opens a pitch or answer with *"I'm sorry to take your time..."*, hit an immediate freeze:
     > *"Freeze! Never apologize for being in the room. You were invited because you have value to offer. Replace 'I'm sorry' with: 'Thank you for prioritizing this discussion.' Let's try that again!"*

---

### B. Phonetic & Prosodic Mechanics for Webcams
1. **The Consonant Guillotine (Vowel Epenthesis)**:
   - Japanese has no final consonants (except /n/). Japanese speakers add /u/ or /o/ to English stops: *pitch ➔ pitch-u*, *product ➔ prod-uc-to*, *client ➔ clien-to*.
   - **The Drill**: Instruct the student: *"Put your finger on your throat. When you say 'pitch', stop your breath instantly. Zero sound after the 'ch'. /pɪtʃ/. Snap the guillotine!"*

2. **Downward Declarative Inflection (Banning "Uptalk")**:
   - Hesitant speakers end statements with rising pitch (like a question: *"We have three features? ↗"*). This signals insecurity.
   - **The Drill**: Model the **Executive Downward Step**: Drop pitch on the final syllable of the sentence (*"We have three pillars. ↘"*). It creates finality, gravitas, and unshakeable authority.

3. **Mora-Timed Monotone vs. Stress-Timed Punch**:
   - Japanese gives equal length to every syllable. English punches the tonic content words and squashes the rest.
   - **The Drill**: Use physical gestures. Have the student tap their fist on the desk or clap their hands on key words:
     - *"We **SLASH** deployment time by **FORTY** percent."*

## 4. Adapting Oren Klaff's *Pitch Anything* for English Language Learners

Oren Klaff’s groundbreaking book *Pitch Anything* revolves around how the human brain actually processes pitches. Here is how you translate these neuro-pitching concepts into concrete ESL coaching:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            THE CROC BRAIN VS. THE NEOCORTEX IN ESL PITCHING                       |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  THE CROCODILE BRAIN (Buyer's Filter)             |  THE NEOCORTEX (Where Most ESL Students Pitch)|
|  - Primitive, survival-focused, easily bored      |  - Logic, complex data, grammar rules         |
|  - Hates complexity, loves clear contrasts        |  - Overloads the buyer with technical detail  |
|  - Responds to NOVELTY, STATUS, and EMOTION       |  - Results in polite nodding and ZERO sales   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. The Crocodile Brain (Simplify and Hook)
- **Concept**: The buyer’s brain discards 90% of what is said if it sounds like abstract jargon or a dense lecture.
- **How to Teach**: Train your student to open with a **Macro Shift** (what changed in the world) and a **Tension Hook** (why the old way is failing) before talking about product features.

### 2. Setting the Frame & Status Elevation
- **Concept**: In any meeting, whoever controls the frame controls the outcome.
- **The Beta Trap**: Walking into a meeting thanking the client profusely, waiting nervously while they check their phone, and accepting arbitrary time cuts (*"You only have 5 minutes"*).
- **The Executive Frame**: Establishing equal status immediately:
  - *Time Frame*: *"I actually have a hard stop at 25 minutes past the hour, so let’s dive right into the core numbers."*
  - *The Prize Frame*: Positioning yourself as the prize: *"Today isn't about convincing you; it’s about determining whether our technical infrastructure is the right fit for your team's roadmap."*

### 3. Eradicating Neediness
- Neediness is the #1 deal killer. In language learning, neediness sounds like:
  - *"Does that make sense?"* ➔ Replace with: *"How does that align with your current priorities?"*
  - *"I hope this is okay."* ➔ Replace with: *"That is our recommended approach."*
  - *"Sorry for my English."* ➔ **BANNED completely from your classroom.**

## 5. Real-Time Classroom Correction Protocols

When teaching high-stakes business communication, how you correct errors determines whether the student grows or shuts down.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  THE 3-TIER ERROR CORRECTION SYSTEM                                |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Level 1: THE INVISIBLE RECAST        | Level 2: CHAT BOX SIGNAL          | Level 3: THE HARD RESET  |
| (For minor slips during flow)        | (For vocabulary upgrades)         | (For catastrophic habits)|
| Repeat the thought back naturally    | Type upgrade silently in chat     | Stop student immediately |
| using the correct native phrase.     | while they finish speaking.       | and redo the opener.     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Protocol 1: The Invisible Recast (Flow Preserved)
- **Student**: *"Our software make the data very fast to search."*
- **Tutor**: *"Right—so the software **accelerates data retrieval significantly**. What does that mean for the end-user?"*
- *Result*: The student hears the native executive phrase modeled in context without experiencing interruption or embarrassment.

### Protocol 2: The Chat-Box Signal (Lexical Elevation)
- While the student is giving an answer, silently type in the chat box:
  - `make better ➔ streamline / optimize`
- Gently point at your screen. The student catches the word with their peripheral vision and incorporates it on the spot.

### Protocol 3: The Hard Reset (Habit Elimination)
- Reserve this for the 3 fatal pitch habits:
  1. Apologizing (*"I'm sorry..."*)
  2. Filler stalling for >5 seconds (*"Ano... eto... uhm..."*)
  3. Uptalk question inflection on declarative facts
- **How to Execute**:
  > *"Freeze! Reset the clock. You started with an apology. Sit up tall, drop your shoulders, look into the camera, and start with: 'The core challenge in your supply chain today is...' In 3... 2... 1... Go!"*

## 6. How to Facilitate Roleplays: The "Pushback Dial"

During Phase 4 (Workplace Simulation Lab), you are not just a teacher; you are an actor playing the student's counterpart.

Use the **Pushback Dial** to calibrate the challenge:
```
[Level 1: Receptive Client] ────► [Level 2: Skeptical Stakeholder] ────► [Level 3: Boardroom Shark]
- Friendly & nodding             - Asks for ROI proof                   - Checks phone, interrupts
- Allows student full time       - Questions timeline & cost            - Challenges underlying premise
```

- **Lesson 1**: Start at **Level 1.5** (warm but expects crisp clarity).
- **Lesson 2 & 3**: Push to **Level 2** (skeptical, asks *"So what?"*, challenges metrics).
- **Lesson 4**: Push to **Level 2.5–3** (objects to pricing, tests frame control, tests whether the student holds their ground).
