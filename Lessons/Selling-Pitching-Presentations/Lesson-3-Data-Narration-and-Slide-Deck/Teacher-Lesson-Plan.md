# Lesson 3: Teacher Lesson Plan
## Executive Storytelling & Data Narration
### Transforming Dry Numbers into Compelling Business Narratives & Boardroom Persuasion

- **Module**: Selling, Pitching & Business Presentations (Lesson 3 of 4)
- **Student**: YH (Conversational B1–B2 ➔ Executive B2/C1)
- **Tutor**: Lewis O. | **Platform**: Preply
- **Duration**: 50 Minutes
- **Key Reference Text**: *Pitch Anything* (The Intrigue Frame, Narrative Tension & Visual Cognition)
- **Core Objective**: Teach YH to transition from passive slide-reading to persuasive executive data narration using the NMTA Model (Number-Meaning-Trajectory-Action), master contrastive numerical stress, and command high-stakes Q&A during data presentations.

---

## 1. Lesson Objectives
By the end of this 50-minute session, YH will:
1. **Deploy the NMTA Data Narration Formula**:
   - **N**umber (State the metric crisply)
   - **M**eaning (Explain the business significance)
   - **T**rajectory (Describe where the trend is heading)
   - **A**ction (Propose the executive strategic decision)
2. **Master Dynamic Trajectory Verbs**: Replace elementary data verbs (*went up, went down, was flat*) with vivid boardroom verbs (*surged, plummeted, plateaued, outpaced, stagnated*).
3. **Execute Contrastive Numerical Stress**: Use vocal pitch and volume contrast to highlight opposing financial or operational trends (*"Costs dropped by **20%**, while throughput surged by **45%**"*).
4. **Build Intrigue & Narrative Tension**: Apply Oren Klaff’s Intrigue Frame to present a complex business case study with narrative suspense before revealing the solution.
5. **Handle Skeptical Boardroom Inquiries**: Confidently defend slide projections without sounding defensive or apologetic.

## 3. Step-by-Step Facilitation & Exercise Bank

### Phase 1: Executive Warm-Up & Anti-Translation Sprint (00:00 – 08:00)

#### Exercise 1.1: The 30-Second "Trendline Speed Sprint" (4 mins)
*Facilitation: Lewis calls out a data movement. YH has 3 seconds to describe it using an executive trajectory verb and one business reason!*

1. **Lewis**: *"User signups in Q3 went from 1,000 to 10,000 in one month."*
   - **YH Response**: *"In Q3, user acquisition **surged ten-fold**, outpacing our initial quarterly forecast due to viral enterprise adoption."*
2. **Lewis**: *"Customer churn was 12% in January and is now 2% in September."*
   - **YH Response**: *"Over the past nine months, our churn rate **plummeted to an unprecedented 2%**, reflecting superior product stickiness."*
3. **Lewis**: *"Sales stayed at $5M for four straight quarters."*
   - **YH Response**: *"Revenue has **plateaued at $5 million**, indicating our legacy channels have reached market saturation."*
4. **Lewis**: *"Our cloud infrastructure server costs doubled last quarter."*
   - **YH Response**: *"Infrastructure expenditures **escalated sharply**, signaling an urgent need to optimize our backend capacity."*

#### Exercise 1.2: Banning "Look at this slide" (4 mins)
*Facilitation: Have YH practice 3 sophisticated slide transition starters:*
- Opener 1: *"If you direct your attention to the upper quadrant..."*
- Opener 2: *"What this data illustrates is a fundamental divergence between..."*
- Opener 3: *"The critical takeaway from this visual is..."*

### Phase 2: Core Concept & Delivery Clinic (08:00 – 20:00)

#### A. The Pedagogy: The NMTA Data Narration Formula
Explain to YH why executives hate raw numbers:
> *"YH, executives don't care about numbers. They care about what numbers **mean for their future**. If you say 'Our revenue was 15 million', that is just raw noise to the Croc Brain. To persuade, you must use the NMTA Formula:"*

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  THE NMTA DATA NARRATION FORMULA                                  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. NUMBER     | "Our customer retention rate reached 92% this past quarter."                       |
| 2. MEANING    | "This positions our solution in the top 5th percentile of enterprise SaaS."        |
| 3. TRAJECTORY | "At this current velocity, we are on track to outpace our primary competitor by Q4."|
| 4. ACTION     | "Consequently, we recommend reallocating $200k into our customer success team."   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### B. Oren Klaff’s "Intrigue Frame" for Case Studies
Teach YH how to tell a 60-second client success story:
1. **The Tension**: *"Six months ago, Client X was facing a potential catastrophic failure: their deployment pipeline was failing 30% of builds."*
2. **The Obstacle**: *"Every vendor they consulted told them an overhaul would take nine months and cost $1.2M."*
3. **The Breakthrough**: *"We introduced our automated routing layer. Within two weeks, build failures dropped to zero, saving their Q4 launch."*

#### C. Vocal Mechanics: Contrastive Numerical Stress
- Model the vocal punch:
  - ❌ Flat/Mora-timed: *"Costs went down twenty percent and revenue went up forty percent."*
  - 💎 Contrastive Dynamic: *"While operational costs **DROPPED by twenty percent** ↘, top-line revenue **SURGED by forty-five percent**! ↗"*
- Have YH mirror your vocal melody 3 times.

### Phase 3: 3-Tier Executive Lexical Upgrade (20:00 – 32:00)

| Elementary (Tier 1) | Professional (Tier 2) | Executive Impact (Tier 3) | Contextual Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Look at this chart* | *As you can see here* | **As illustrated in this comparative breakdown...** | Anchors executive visual focus. |
| *Went up very fast* | *Grew quickly* | **Experienced exponential growth / Surged...** | Expresses aggressive velocity. |
| *Went down quickly* | *Decreased significantly* | **Plummeted / Contracted sharply...** | Dramatic cost or risk reduction. |
| *Stayed the same* | *Didn't change* | **Plateaued / Reached equilibrium...** | Signals maturity or stagnation. |
| *Did better than others*| *Beat the competitors* | **Consistently outpaced industry benchmarks...** | Shows market leadership. |
| *We should do this* | *I recommend this plan* | **This warrants an immediate reallocation of resources.** | Decisive board-level recommendation. |

### Phase 4: Workplace Simulation Lab (32:00 – 45:00)
> **TEACHER ACTION**: **Turn OFF screen share!** Simulate a live executive boardroom.

#### Simulation Scenario: The Impatient Boardroom Member (13 mins)
- **Teacher Persona**: *Evelyn Reed*, Managing Director / Board Member. Sharp, numbers-driven, skeptical of rosy forecasts.
- **Context**: YH is presenting a slide showing a 40% efficiency gain and requesting budget approval for an enterprise rollout.
- **Opening Challenge (Lewis)**:
  > *"YH, these efficiency graphs look great on paper, but I’ve seen twenty vendors bring me identical hockey-stick projections. What is actually driving these numbers, and why should the board believe this trajectory is sustainable?"*
- **Target Student Behavior**:
  - Maintains calm vocal cadence (no apologetic *"Sumimasen / I'm sorry"*).
  - Delivers NMTA structure with confidence.
  - Integrates contrastive numerical stress.
  - Deploys the Intrigue Frame with a concrete pilot case study.
- **Evaluation Points**:
  - Did he explain the *Meaning* and *Action*, or just repeat the *Number*?
  - Did he use dynamic verbs (*outpace, surge, plateau*)?
  - Was his eye contact locked on the camera?
