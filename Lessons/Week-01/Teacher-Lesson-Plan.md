# Week 1: Teacher Lesson Plan
## Executive Meeting Agility & The High-Impact Vocabulary Upgrade
### Breaking the Katakana Trap, High-Impact Action Verbs, PREP Framework & Spontaneous Meeting Delivery

- **Student**: YH (Self-assessed Conversational / Intermediate B1–B2)
- **Tutor**: Lewis O.
- **Duration**: 50 Minutes
- **Target Accent & Model**: International Business English (Clear, standard, professional prosody)
- **Core Pedagogical Objective**: Purge Katakana loanword habits, expand generic vocabulary into Tier-3 executive business vernacular, and train spontaneous, confident contributions in meetings using the PREP model.

---

## Lesson Objectives
By the end of this 50-minute session, YH will:
1. **Purge Katakana English (*Wasei-Eigo*)**: Identify and replace 5 high-frequency Katakana loanwords (*claim*, *cospa*, *appoint*, *remake*, *NG*) with authentic international business equivalents (*lodged a complaint*, *cost-effective / high ROI*, *schedule a sync*, *overhaul/revise*, *non-viable/out of scope*).
2. **Execute Crisp Consonant Closures**: Eliminate vowel epenthesis (adding trailing /u/ and /o/) on terminal consonants in high-frequency business nouns (*risk*, *client*, *task*, *project*, *feedback*).
3. **Master 2-Syllable Stress Shifts**: Differentiate noun stress (1st syllable) from verb stress (2nd syllable) across core business words (*REC-ord* vs. *re-CORD*, *PROJ-ect* vs. *pro-JECT*, *PRES-ent* vs. *pre-SENT*).
4. **Deploy Tier-3 Executive Collocations**: Spontaneously use at least 4 advanced business collocations (*streamline operations*, *mitigate risks*, *critical bottleneck*, *align on expectations*) during a live workplace simulation.
5. **Structure Meeting Responses with PREP**: Deliver a succinct, assertive 60-second update or opinion without meandering or falling into Japanese apologetic hesitation.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Instructions & Exercise Banks

### Phase 1: Executive Warm-Up & Vocabulary Retrieval Sprint (00:00 – 08:00)
**Tutor Goal**: Shake off the workday, deactivate the Japanese translation loop, eliminate *"Ano..."* and *"Eto..."* filler stalls, and wake up active lexical retrieval.

#### Exercise 1.1: Rapid-Fire Associative Sprint ("Define What It Is") (4–5 mins)
*Rule: Lewis calls out a trigger word or concept; YH has exactly 3 seconds to begin speaking and must speak for 20–30 seconds continuously without stopping, repeating words, or asking for translations. His objective is to define what it is, give a quick real-world contrast, or describe an everyday workplace/life situation where it happens.*

##### A. The 10-Word Practical Business & Everyday Trigger Bank
1. **Trigger 1: *A Bottleneck*** (`/ˈbɒt.əl.nek/`)
   - **What It Is**: A point of congestion or delay where an entire process, project, or traffic flow slows down because one specific stage cannot keep up.
   - *Collocations*: *critical bottleneck*, *identify the bottleneck*, *alleviate the bottleneck*, *bottleneck in the workflow*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It's essentially a choke point where everything gets stuck because one person or system is too slow."*
2. **Trigger 2: *Bandwidth*** (`/ˈbænd.wɪdθ/`)
   - **What It Is**: The mental capacity, time, or energy a person or team has available to take on additional tasks or responsibilities.
   - *Collocations*: *limited bandwidth*, *lack the bandwidth*, *free up bandwidth*, *zero bandwidth today*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It means having the free time and mental energy to handle more work without burning out."*
3. **Trigger 3: *A Trade-off*** (`/ˈtreɪd.ɒf/`)
   - **What It Is**: A situational compromise where you accept giving up one desirable benefit in order to gain another (e.g. speed vs. quality, cost vs. convenience).
   - *Collocations*: *a necessary trade-off*, *weigh up the trade-offs*, *an acceptable trade-off*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It's when you choose between two good things—like getting something done fast versus getting it done perfectly."*
4. **Trigger 4: *A Workaround*** (`/ˈwɜːk.ə.raʊnd/`)
   - **What It Is**: A temporary, clever method or shortcut used to bypass a bug or problem when the official system or tool isn't functioning.
   - *Collocations*: *a temporary workaround*, *find a quick workaround*, *clever workaround*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It's a temporary fix you use when the main system fails so you can keep working."*
5. **Trigger 5: *Out of the loop vs. In the loop*** (`/luːp/`)
   - **What It Is**: The difference between being uninformed/excluded from updates versus being kept actively informed of decisions and progress.
   - *Collocations*: *keep me in the loop*, *feel completely out of the loop*, *loop someone in*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"Being out of the loop means you don't know what's happening because nobody forwarded the email or invited you."*
6. **Trigger 6: *Scope Creep*** (`/skəʊp kriːp/`)
   - **What It Is**: When a project, task, or home chore gradually expands beyond its agreed boundaries, adding unplanned extra work without extra time or budget.
   - *Collocations*: *prevent scope creep*, *uncontrolled scope creep*, *victim of scope creep*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It's when a client asks for 'just one small extra feature', and suddenly your 1-week project takes a month."*
7. **Trigger 7: *A Blind Spot*** (`/ˈblaɪnd spɒt/`)
   - **What It Is**: An area or topic where someone lacks awareness, information, or objectivity—in business strategy, driving, or personal blind spots.
   - *Collocations*: *critical blind spot*, *have a blind spot for*, *uncover a blind spot*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It's a hidden flaw or risk that you don't notice yourself, but someone looking from the outside sees clearly."*
8. **Trigger 8: *A Handoff*** (`/ˈhænd.ɒf/`)
   - **What It Is**: The smooth transfer of responsibility, data, or project ownership from one person, shift, or department to the next.
   - *Collocations*: *smooth handoff*, *clean handoff*, *design-to-development handoff*, *messy handoff*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It's like passing the baton in a relay race—handing over the project files so the next team can continue seamlessly."*
9. **Trigger 9: *Micromanagement*** (`/ˈmaɪ.krəʊˌmæn.ɪdʒ.mənt/`)
   - **What It Is**: When a manager, boss, or colleague excessively monitors and controls every tiny detail instead of trusting people to do their jobs.
   - *Collocations*: *hate micromanagement*, *prone to micromanage*, *avoid micromanaging*.
   - *Example definition idea*: *"It's when your manager checks every single email you write and asks for updates every thirty minutes."*
10. **Trigger 10: *The Downside (vs. The Upside)*** (`/ˈdaʊn.saɪd/`)
    - **What It Is**: The negative aspect, hidden risk, or disadvantage of a plan or decision compared to the positive benefits (upside).
    - *Collocations*: *weigh upside vs downside*, *the main downside*, *downside risk*.
    - *Example definition idea*: *"The upside is we save money; the downside is delivery will take two weeks longer."*

##### B. The 3-Angle Definition Formula (How YH Should Answer on the Spot)
*Coach YH to anchor his explanation immediately using one of these three sentence starters:*
- **Angle 1 (Functional Meaning)**: *"It essentially refers to a situation where..."*
- **Angle 2 (Contrast & Counterpart)**: *"It's practically the opposite of [X]; instead of [Y], it means..."*
- **Angle 3 (Everyday Workplace Vignette)**: *"For example, imagine you are at work and your manager / client... that's a classic example of it."*

*Tutor Coaching Note*: If YH pauses looking at the ceiling for more than 3 seconds or says *"Ano..."*, interrupt gently: *"Don't hunt for a single Japanese word! Start with Angle 1: 'It essentially refers to...' Go!"*

### Phase 2: Katakana Deconstruction & Phonetic Clinic (08:00 – 18:00)
**Tutor Goal**: Physically train YH's mouth to close cleanly on consonants (eliminating vowel epenthesis) and master English stress-timing.

#### Exercise 2.1: The "Consonant Guillotine" (Silencing Epenthetic Vowels) (5 mins)
*The Japanese phonetic system (mora) attaches vowels (/u/ or /o/) to almost every consonant. In business meetings, saying "risuku" or "cliento" sounds juvenile and slows down speech.*

- **Mouth Mechanics Coaching**:
  1. Have YH place his fingertips lightly on his throat/larynx.
  2. Explain: *"In Japanese, your vocal cords vibrate until the very end because of the vowel. In English, the consonant is a physical wall. The air cuts off instantly."*
  3. Model the contrast:
     - ❌ *risu-ku* ➔ ✅ **Risk** (Crisp /k/ unreleased closure, tongue back of palate, silent release).
     - ❌ *clien-to* ➔ ✅ **Client** (Tongue tip seals alveolar ridge for /t/, zero vowel sound after).
     - ❌ *tasu-ku* ➔ ✅ **Task** (Crisp /sk/ cluster).
     - ❌ *feedo-bakku* ➔ ✅ **Feedback** (Stop at /d/, crisp /k/).
     - ❌ *pro-jec-to* ➔ ✅ **Project** (Crisp /t/).
     - ❌ *con-trac-to* ➔ ✅ **Contract** (Crisp /kt/).

- **The Rapid Contrast Drill**:
  - Tutor: *"Say: 'High risk, key client'."* (Listen closely for *risuku / cliento*).
  - YH repeats 3 times, accelerating speed while maintaining sharp consonant closures.

---

#### Exercise 2.2: 2-Syllable Business Word Stress Shift (5 mins)
*In English, word stress changes the grammatical function completely. Stressing the wrong syllable is one of the main reasons non-native speakers are misunderstood in boardroom presentations.*

- **The Rule**:
  - **NOUNS** = Stress on the **1st Syllable** (LOUD, higher pitch, elongated vowel).
  - **VERBS** = Stress on the **2nd Syllable** (Weak first syllable /ə/, punch on the second).

| Word | Noun Pronunciation (1st Syllable Stressed) | Verb Pronunciation (2nd Syllable Stressed) | Practice Contrast Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Project** | /ˈprɒdʒ.ekt/ (*PROJ-ect*) | /prəˈdʒekt/ (*pro-JECT*) | *"We need to **pro-JECT** the budget for this new **PROJ-ect**."* |
| **Record** | /ˈrek.ɔːd/ (*REC-ord*) | /rɪˈkɔːd/ (*re-CORD*) | *"Please **re-CORD** the meeting to keep a clear **REC-ord**."* |
| **Present** | /ˈprez.ənt/ (*PRES-ent*) | /prɪˈzent/ (*pre-SENT*) | *"I will **pre-SENT** the quarterly data at the **PRES-ent** moment."* |
| **Contract** | /ˈkɒn.trækt/ (*CON-tract*) | /kənˈtrækt/ (*con-TRACT*) | *"If the market **con-TRACTS**, we won't sign the **CON-tract**."* |
| **Conduct** | /ˈkɒn.dʌkt/ (*CON-duct*) | /kənˈdʌkt/ (*con-DUCT*) | *"We will **con-DUCT** a formal audit of corporate **CON-duct**."* |

*Tutor Coaching Cue*: Tap the desk firmly on the stressed syllable. Exaggerate the pitch jump on the stressed syllable so YH can hear and mimic the melody.

### Phase 3: The 3-Tier Executive Vocabulary Matrix & Collocation Vault (18:00 – 32:00)
**Tutor Goal**: Introduce 6 high-yield business collocations and phrasal verbs, dissect their precise workplace nuances, and force YH to apply them to his actual work.

#### The 6 Core Executive Targets for Today

1. **Streamline operations / workflows** (`/ˈstriːm.laɪn/`)
   - *Meaning*: To simplify a process by removing unnecessary steps, paperwork, or friction to save time and capital.
   - *Contrast with Basic*: *"Make the work faster/easier"* ➔ *"Streamline operations"*.
   - *Japanese Nuance*: 業務を効率化する・無駄を省く (not just "speed up").
   - *Collocations*: *streamline our workflow*, *streamline the approval process*, *streamline onboarding*.

2. **Mitigate risks / fallout** (`/ˈmɪt.ɪ.ɡeɪt/`)
   - *Meaning*: To take proactive measures to reduce the severity, danger, or financial damage of an issue before it escalates.
   - *Contrast with Basic*: *"Reduce the bad things"* ➔ *"Mitigate the risks"*.
   - *Japanese Nuance*: リスクを軽減する・最小限に抑える.
   - *Collocations*: *mitigate potential risks*, *mitigate downside exposure*, *take steps to mitigate*.

3. **Critical bottleneck / blocker** (`/ˈbɒt.əl.nek/`)
   - *Meaning*: A specific stage or constraint in a project that slows down the entire progress.
   - *Contrast with Basic*: *"Big problem / slow point"* ➔ *"Critical bottleneck"*.
   - *Japanese Nuance*: ボトルネック・進行の最大の障害.
   - *Collocations*: *identify the bottleneck*, *alleviate the bottleneck*, *unblock team bottlenecks*.

4. **Align on expectations / deliverables** (`/əˈlaɪn/`)
   - *Meaning*: To ensure all team members and stakeholders have the exact same understanding of goals, timelines, and responsibilities.
   - *Contrast with Basic*: *"Check that we agree"* ➔ *"Align on expectations"*.
   - *Japanese Nuance*: 認識を合わせる・コンセンサスを取る (much more professional than "agree").
   - *Collocations*: *align on priorities*, *ensure leadership is aligned*, *get everyone aligned*.

5. **Iron out the details / kinks** (`/ˈaɪən aʊt/`)
   - *Meaning*: To resolve minor problems, friction points, or technical ambiguities before finalizing a deal or release.
   - *Contrast with Basic*: *"Fix small issues"* ➔ *"Iron out the kinks"*.
   - *Japanese Nuance*: 細かい問題を詰める・すり合わせる.
   - *Collocations*: *iron out contract terms*, *iron out final discrepancies*, *iron out the wrinkles*.

6. **Touch base / Circle back** (`/tʌtʃ beɪs/` / /ˈsɜː.kəl bæk/)
   - *Meaning*: To briefly check in with someone or revisit a topic once more information is available.
   - *Contrast with Basic*: *"Talk again later"* ➔ *"Touch base / circle back next week"*.
   - *Japanese Nuance*: 状況を確認する・改めて連絡する.
   - *Collocations*: *touch base offline*, *touch base briefly on Friday*, *circle back with updated metrics*.

#### Micro-Production Drill (6 mins)
*Have YH generate 1 real-world sentence for each target, based on his real projects or workplace meetings. Lewis gently refines tone and preposition accuracy.*

### Phase 4: Workplace Simulation Lab & Roleplay (32:00 – 46:00)
**Tutor Goal**: Put YH in realistic corporate scenarios requiring spontaneous deployment of the new vocabulary and structured communication using the PREP Framework.

#### The PREP Framework Formula (Quick 1-Minute Calibration)
- **P (Point)**: State your main conclusion or stance in 1 punchy sentence.
- **R (Reason)**: Provide the underlying business justification or operational rationale.
- **E (Example / Evidence)**: Cite a real metric, previous case, or concrete observation.
- **P (Point)**: Restate the takeaway or propose the clear next action.

---

#### Simulation Scenario 1: The Urgent Cross-Functional Sprint Sync (7 mins)
- **Context**: YH is leading/coordinating a project update. Lewis plays *Marcus*, a stressed Product Manager from another department who is worried about missing the Q4 launch deadline.
- **Marcus (Lewis)**: *"YH, thanks for getting on the call. Look, engineering is saying the client dashboard won't be ready by the 15th, and leadership is breathing down my neck. What's going on with your team's deliverables?"*
- **YH's Objective**:
  - Open using an **Executive Bridge** (do NOT say *"I'm sorry"* or panic).
  - Use **PREP** to explain the situation clearly.
  - Mandatory vocabulary to incorporate: *critical bottleneck*, *streamline*, *mitigate risks*, *align on expectations*.
- **Model Student Delivery**:
  - *(Point)*: *"I hear your concern, Marcus. Our top priority is ensuring we meet the launch date without compromising quality."*
  - *(Reason)*: *"Right now, the third-party API integration is our **critical bottleneck**, which has delayed testing by two days."*
  - *(Example)*: *"To **mitigate the risk** of slippage, we reallocated two senior engineers yesterday to **streamline** the data pipeline."*
  - *(Point)*: *"Let's **align on expectations** with leadership today: the core feature will be locked in by Friday, and we'll **circle back** with a final status update tomorrow morning."*

*Tutor Coaching Note*: If YH falls back into passive Japanese apologetic phrasing (*"I'm so sorry, engineering has big trouble..."*), interject as Marcus: *"Don't apologize, YH—give me the solution. Where is the bottleneck and how do we solve it?"* This trains professional executive ownership.

---

#### Simulation Scenario 2: Client Expectation Management & Diplomatic Pushback (7 mins)
- **Context**: Lewis plays *David Chen*, an aggressive international enterprise client who suddenly demands three major additional features added to the contract with zero extra budget and no deadline extension (scope creep).
- **David (Lewis)**: *"Look YH, our executives reviewed the demo, and we need the automated reporting module and custom CRM sync included before we sign off next Monday. I assume your team can include that within the original quote, right?"*
- **YH's Objective**:
  - Stand ground diplomatically without sounding blunt or defensive.
  - Offer a win-win alternative or conditional trade-off.
  - Mandatory vocabulary to incorporate: *out of scope / non-viable*, *iron out*, *cost-effective*, *touch base*.
- **Model Student Delivery**:
  - *"I completely appreciate why automated reporting is attractive for your leadership team, David."*
  - *"However, based on our initial specifications, those features are currently **out of scope** for the initial rollout."*
  - *"If we try to squeeze them into this sprint, we risk diluting platform stability. What we can do is **iron out the details** for Phase 1 as planned, deliver on time next week, and present a **cost-effective** add-on package for Phase 2."*
  - *"Let me send over the revised scope breakdown so we can **touch base** tomorrow afternoon to finalize the sign-off."*
