## 1. Anti-Translation Sprint: The "Define What It Is" Speed Matrix
### (Practical Business & Everyday Workplace Triggers)

> **Core Objective**: Stop translating from Japanese in your head! When you hear a concept word, you have **3 seconds** to open your mouth and speak for 20–30 seconds. Do not hunt for a dictionary translation—define what it is, explain when it happens, or give a real-world example in simple, fluid English.

### A. The 10 High-Yield Concept Triggers

| Concept Trigger | Core Meaning (What It Is) | Key Collocations & Everyday Context | Natural Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A Bottleneck** (`/ˈbɒt.əl.nek/`) | A point of congestion or delay where an entire workflow slows down because one specific stage cannot keep up. | *critical bottleneck*, *identify the bottleneck*, *bottleneck in the workflow* | *"The legal team review is our main bottleneck right now."* |
| **Bandwidth** (`/ˈbænd.wɪdθ/`) | Mental capacity, energy, or free time available to take on more work or personal tasks. | *limited bandwidth*, *free up bandwidth*, *have zero bandwidth today* | *"I'd love to help with the new demo, but I lack the bandwidth this week."* |
| **A Trade-off** (`/ˈtreɪd.ɒf/`) | Giving up one desirable benefit in order to gain another (e.g. speed vs. quality, cost vs. convenience). | *a necessary trade-off*, *weigh up the trade-offs*, *fair trade-off* | *"Going with a cheaper vendor is a trade-off against delivery speed."* |
| **A Workaround** (`/ˈwɜːk.ə.raʊnd/`) | A temporary, clever fix or shortcut used to bypass a bug or broken system so work can continue. | *a temporary workaround*, *find a quick workaround*, *clever workaround* | *"The printer network is down, so our workaround is emailing PDFs directly."* |

## 2. The Katakana-to-Executive Replacement Matrix

> **Core Objective**: Stop relying on Japanese-English (*Wasei-Eigo*) words that cause confusion in international business. Upgrade immediately to standard global corporate terminology.

| Japanese Katakana Phrase | Common Confusion / Misunderstanding | International Executive Upgrade | Natural Workplace Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Claim (クレーム)** | *Claim* means to assert or demand money. It does not mean customer complaint. | **Lodge a complaint**, **raise an issue**, **escalate a concern** | *"The client lodged a formal complaint regarding data sync delays."* |
| **Cost-performance (コスパ)** | Unknown or confusing to Western and international colleagues. | **Cost-effective**, **high ROI (Return on Investment)**, **bang for the buck** | *"This cloud architecture is far more cost-effective over a 3-year horizon."* |
| **Appo / Appoint (アポ)** | *Appoint* is a verb for naming a director; not a noun for a meeting. | **Schedule a sync**, **lock in a meeting**, **send a calendar invite** | *"Let's schedule a 15-minute sync with the design team tomorrow."* |
| **Remake (リメイク)** | Exclusively used for movies or pop songs. | **Overhaul**, **revise**, **re-architect**, **redesign** | *"We need to overhaul the onboarding slide deck before pitching to the board."* |
| **NG (エヌジー)** | Casual abbreviation largely unknown outside East Asia. | **Non-viable**, **out of scope**, **off the table**, **disqualified** | *"Adding custom code at this stage is completely out of scope."* |
| **Sign (サイン)** | Usually refers to a physical signpost or billboard. | **Sign-off**, **secure client signature**, **execute the agreement** | *"We are waiting for final sign-off from the legal department."* |
| **Consent (コンセント)** | Means legal permission or approval. | **Power outlet**, **electrical socket**, **docking station** | *"Does the conference room have enough power outlets for the laptops?"* |
| **Salaryman (サラリーマン)** | Culturally Japanese term. | **Corporate professional**, **knowledge worker**, **full-time employee** | *"Our software is tailored specifically for corporate knowledge workers."* |

## 4. Pronunciation & Articulatory Precision

### A. The "Consonant Guillotine": Chopping Epenthetic Vowels
In Japanese phonetics, consonants almost always end with a vowel sound (/u/ or /o/). In English, consonants must end with a crisp, physical stop:

- ❌ *risu-ku* ➔ ✅ **Risk** (Cut the air off at the back of the tongue on /k/).
- ❌ *clien-to* ➔ ✅ **Client** (Firm tongue tip tap against the alveolar ridge for /t/).
- ❌ *tasu-ku* ➔ ✅ **Task** (Crisp /sk/ cluster with zero vowel echo).
- ❌ *pro-jec-to* ➔ ✅ **Project** (Clean terminal /t/ stop).
- ❌ *feedo-bakku* ➔ ✅ **Feedback** (Sharp /k/ release).
- ❌ *con-trac-to* ➔ ✅ **Contract** (Clean /kt/ blend).

---

### B. The 2-Syllable Business Word Stress Shift
Word stress changes the grammatical role of the word:
- **NOUN = 1st Syllable Stressed** (*PROJ-ect*, *REC-ord*, *PRES-ent*, *CON-tract*).
- **VERB = 2nd Syllable Stressed** (*pro-JECT*, *re-CORD*, *pre-SENT*, *con-TRACT*).

| Word | Part of Speech | Stressed Syllable | Audio Cadence Pattern | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Project** | Noun | **1st** (`/ˈprɒdʒ.ekt/`) | **DUM**-da | *"Who is the lead on this new **PROJ-ect**?"* |
| **Project** | Verb | **2nd** (`/prəˈdʒekt/`) | da-**DUM** | *"We **pro-JECT** a 15% bump in revenue next quarter."* |
| **Record** | Noun | **1st** (`/ˈrek.ɔːd/`) | **DUM**-da | *"Keep a detailed **REC-ord** of client correspondence."* |
| **Record** | Verb | **2nd** (`/rɪˈkɔːd/`) | da-**DUM** | *"Please **re-CORD** the product demo for missing team members."* |
| **Present** | Noun / Adj | **1st** (`/ˈprez.ənt/`) | **DUM**-da | *"Everyone is in the room for the **PRES-ent** meeting."* |
| **Present** | Verb | **2nd** (`/prɪˈzent/`) | da-**DUM** | *"I'd like to **pre-SENT** our Q3 milestone roadmap."* |

## 5. The Executive Meeting Collocation Vault

Master these 6 high-yield phrases for your daily meetings, teamwork syncs, and client calls:

### 1. Streamline operations / workflows
- **Meaning**: To optimize a process by eliminating waste, redundancies, and unnecessary manual steps.
- **Japanese Equivalent**: 業務効率化・プロセスの合理化.
- **Natural Collocations**: *streamline operations*, *streamline the approval workflow*, *streamline communication*.
- *Example*: *"By automating our weekly status reports, we streamlined cross-functional operations significantly."*

### 2. Mitigate risks / downside
- **Meaning**: To implement proactive safeguards that reduce the likelihood or impact of potential hazards.
- **Japanese Equivalent**: リスクを低減する・悪影響を抑える.
- **Natural Collocations**: *mitigate financial risks*, *mitigate regulatory exposure*, *proactively mitigate*.
- *Example*: *"We introduced automated regression tests to mitigate deployment risks before the major release."*

### 3. Critical bottleneck / blocker
- **Meaning**: A specific constraint or blockage that restricts overall throughput and holds up progress.
- **Japanese Equivalent**: ボトルネック・進行を阻害する障害.
- **Natural Collocations**: *identify the bottleneck*, *resolve the bottleneck*, *alleviate production bottlenecks*.
- *Example*: *"The legal review is currently our critical bottleneck; once that clears, deployment will take 48 hours."*

### 4. Align on expectations / deliverables
- **Meaning**: To establish mutual clarity and complete consensus across all team members regarding goals and timelines.
- **Japanese Equivalent**: 認識の統一・目線合わせ・合意形成.
- **Natural Collocations**: *ensure everyone is aligned*, *align on priorities*, *align leadership on strategy*.
- *Example*: *"Before we kick off development, let's schedule a 30-minute sync to align on deliverables."*

### 5. Iron out the details / kinks
- **Meaning**: To work through and settle minor technical issues, discrepancies, or operational friction.
- **Japanese Equivalent**: 細部を詰める・最終調整をする.
- **Natural Collocations**: *iron out contractual terms*, *iron out final discrepancies*, *iron out technical bugs*.
- *Example*: *"The broad agreement is in place; our teams just need to iron out the technical integration details."*

### 6. Touch base / Circle back
- **Meaning**: To make brief contact to exchange an update, or revisit a discussion once new data is available.
- **Japanese Equivalent**: 状況を確認する・後ほど改めて確認する.
- **Natural Collocations**: *touch base offline*, *touch base early next week*, *circle back with updated numbers*.
- *Example*: *"Let me review the revised architecture with engineering, and I'll circle back with you by 3 PM."*

## 6. The PREP Framework for Meetings & Standups

When you speak up in a meeting, avoid starting with hesitation (*"Ano..."* or *"Maybe it could be..."*). Use the **PREP Framework** to deliver crisp, authoritative contributions in 45–60 seconds:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                        THE PREP FRAMEWORK                                         |
+-------------------+--------------------------------------------------+----------------------------+
| Step              | Function                                         | Power Starter Phrase       |
+-------------------+--------------------------------------------------+----------------------------+
| **P** - Point     | Direct conclusion or key opinion in one sentence | *"My main takeaway is..."  |
| **R** - Reason    | Business justification or operational rationale  | *"The primary driver is..."|
| **E** - Example   | Concrete metric, client case, or past project    | *"For instance, when we..."|
| **P** - Point     | Clear next step, takeaway, or call to action     | *"Moving forward, I propose"*
+-------------------+--------------------------------------------------+----------------------------+
```

### Diplomatic Pushback Formulas (Replacing *"I'm sorry but..."*)
In global business, an apology suggests you made an error. Replace apologies with professional boundary markers:

- **Acknowledge & Reframe**:  
  *"I completely see where you're coming from; however, from an engineering feasibility standpoint..."*
- **Highlight Trade-Offs**:  
  *"If we prioritize Feature A for this release, the trade-off is that Feature B will have to shift to Q2."*
- **Conditional Agreement**:  
  *"We would be happy to accommodate that request, contingent upon securing additional design resources."*
