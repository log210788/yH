# LESSON 1: UPWARD MANAGEMENT, ASYMMETRICAL INFORMATION & EXECUTIVE AGENCY

## Activity 1: The Asymmetrical Briefing Audit ("Same Same, But Different")
* **Adapted From**: *Same Same but Different* (Bolen, `split_067`)
* **Time**: 12–15 Minutes | **Skills**: Strategic Reading, Note-Taking, Upward Management (The 70/30 Rule)
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: In Bolen's *Same Same but Different*, the teacher prepares two versions of a text with a set number of strategic changes; the student reads Version A, takes notes, and then audits Version B to identify the exact changes and explain their impact. Here, we apply this to the **70/30 Upward Reporting Rule** from YH's Oct 21 lesson: comparing a junior manager's **100% Routine Update (Version A)** with an executive's **70/30 Strategic Foresight Brief (Version B)** that eliminates information asymmetry.

### Target Word Bank for Activity 1:
`information asymmetry` · `spearhead` · `dilute operational bandwidth` · `onboard high-margin SKUs` · `half-goal circuit breaker` · `latent risk` · `proactive mitigation` · `anticipate priorities`

### Procedure:
1. Show YH **Version A (The Routine Update)** for 60 seconds. Have him jot down the core facts.
2. Reveal **Version B (The 70/30 Executive Brief)** and tell YH: *"There are **5 major strategic & lexical upgrades** between Version A and Version B. Find all 5 and explain why Version B puts the CEO at ease."*

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| VERSION A: The Passive Routine Update (100% Task Reporting — Creates Anxiety)     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| "Hi Boss, this month our team looked at the APAC sales data. We stopped spending  |
| time on useless things and put 30 new items from our good brands on the website.  |
| Sales went up 180% so far. Also, our new warehouse staff are still learning, and  |
| if some bad things happen next month during the holiday rush, we will tell you    |
| right away."                                                                      |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| VERSION B: The 70/30 Executive Brief (Eliminates Information Asymmetry)           |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| "To update you on our APAC Q3 mandate: I spearheaded a catalog audit to ensure    |
| we don't dilute operational bandwidth across low-yield channels. By onboarding 30 |
| high-margin SKUs from our top-performing brand partners, we have driven 180% YoY  |
| revenue growth. Looking ahead to Q4 (30% Risk Foresight): to prevent fulfillment  |
| bottlenecks with our newly onboarded warehouse team, I have installed a half-goal |
| circuit breaker this Friday to audit packing velocity—and we have a backup 3PL    |
| partner on standby so Q4 delivery SLAs remain 100% protected."                    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Teacher Answer Key (The 5 Upgrades to Elicit from YH):
1. **Agency Shift**: *"Our team looked at"* $\rightarrow$ **"I spearheaded a catalog audit"** (claims personal executive ownership).
2. **Resource Precision**: *"Useless things"* $\rightarrow$ **"dilute operational bandwidth across low-yield channels"**.
3. **Retail Vernacular**: *"Put 30 new items from good brands"* $\rightarrow$ **"onboarding 30 high-margin SKUs from top-performing brand partners"**.
4. **Proactive Quality Control**: *"Warehouse staff are still learning"* $\rightarrow$ **"installed a half-goal circuit breaker this Friday to audit packing velocity"**.
5. **Eliminating Asymmetry (The 30% Rule)**: Instead of saying *"if bad things happen, we will tell you"*, Version B **anticipates the CEO's latent risk** (Q4 holiday fulfillment delays) and presents a **pre-engineered mitigation plan** (backup 3PL on standby).

## Activity 2: Executive Collocation Clinic ("Is That Sentence Correct?")
* **Adapted From**: *Is That Sentence Correct?* (Bolen, `split_115`) & *Proofreading Practice* (Bolen, `split_065`)
* **Time**: 10–12 Minutes | **Skills**: Self-Editing, Eliminating L1 Transfer, Register Elevation
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: Bolen recommends mixing 100% natural sentences with sentences containing subtle L1 transfer or register errors. YH must decide if each sentence is **Boardroom-Ready (Correct)** or **Needs an Executive Polish (Incorrect)**, and recast the flawed ones aloud. Every incorrect sentence below is drawn directly from YH's Oct 21 & Oct 23 transcripts or Japanese business transfer habits.

### The 8 Diagnostic Sentences (Share on Screen):
1. *"I always like to see my subordinates grown up through challenging projects."*
   - **Verdict**: ❌ **Incorrect (L1 Collocation Slip)**. *Grow up* is only for children becoming adults.
   - **YH Upgrade**: *"I take pride in **fostering my subordinates' professional development** and watching them **advance** through challenging mandates."*
2. *"By combining qualitative customer context with quantitative transaction data, we validated our core hypothesis."*
   - **Verdict**: ✅ **Boardroom-Ready!** (YH produced this naturally at `13:35` on Oct 21—celebrate it!).
3. *"When we got a big claim from the brand partner about bad cost-performance, we decided to remake our promotion plan."*
   - **Verdict**: ❌ **Incorrect (3 *Wasei-Eigo* traps: claim, cost-performance, remake)**.
   - **YH Upgrade**: *"When the brand partner **escalated a formal concern** regarding **low ROI**, we decided to **overhaul** our promotional strategy."*
4. *"Because I oversee 100 employees across APAC, I structure my span of control around five regional directors and grant them high self-autonomy."*
   - **Verdict**: ✅ **Boardroom-Ready!**
5. *"Once some bad things happen in the supply chain, my team knows they should not hide it."*
   - **Verdict**: ❌ **Incorrect (Tier-1 Elementary Phrasing)**.
   - **YH Upgrade**: *"The moment an **operational bottleneck emerges**, my team knows to **escalate it immediately** alongside **viable mitigation options** rather than sitting on bad news."*
6. *"Please push the white bottom on the right side and see if the mark changes."*
   - **Verdict**: ❌ **Incorrect (Oct 23 Bomb-Defusal Slip: bottom vs. button, mark vs. symbol)**.
   - **YH Upgrade**: *"Please **press and hold the white button** on the right-hand module and confirm whether the **indicator symbol** changes."*
7. *"If we refuse to customize our enterprise package for high-value clients, we are simply leaving money on the table."*
   - **Verdict**: ✅ **Boardroom-Ready!**
8. *"We need to make an appo with the regional leads because there is a big information gap between us."*
   - **Verdict**: ❌ **Incorrect (*Wasei-Eigo* 'appo' + Tier-1 'big information gap')**.
   - **YH Upgrade**: *"We need to **schedule an alignment sync** with our regional leads to resolve the **asymmetrical communication** between headquarters and the field."*

## Activity 5: Executive Leadership Dilemmas ("Would You Rather?" + "Give a Reason")
* **Adapted From**: *Would You Rather?* (Bolen, `split_110`) & *Give a Reason* (Bolen, `split_084`)
* **Time**: 12–15 Minutes | **Skills**: Defending Management Philosophy, Causal & Concessive Discourse Markers
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: Combines *Would You Rather?* (choosing between two tough options) with *Give a Reason* (completing complex causal and consequence clauses). Instead of whimsical questions (*"eyes like a fly or a spider"*), YH faces **3 real organizational dilemmas** tied to managing 100+ staff, meritocracy vs. harmony, and autonomy vs. control.

### Mandatory Causal & Consequence Connectors (Adapted from `split_084`):
- *"I would opt for [Option A] primarily because..."*
- *"While [Option B] delivers short-term speed, the downstream consequence is that..."*
- *"Were I to tolerate [X], it would inevitably erode...; therefore,..."*

### The 3 Executive Leadership Dilemmas:
1. **Dilemma 1 (Meritocracy vs. Combative Culture — from Oct 21 `45:40`)**:
   > **Would you rather** retain a Regional Sales Director who consistently beats revenue quota by 140% but is **combative**, hoards information, and causes high turnover among subordinates—**OR** replace them with a collaborative leader who delivers steady 100% quota and **fosters team autonomy**?
2. **Dilemma 2 (Span of Control vs. Speed)**:
   > When entering a new APAC market (e.g., Vietnam or Thailand), **would you rather** tightly control all SKU selection and pricing yourself for the first 6 months to prevent mistakes—**OR** grant your local Country Manager **100% self-autonomy** from Day 1 accompanied by a **half-goal circuit breaker** at Day 45?
3. **Dilemma 3 (Upward Transparency vs. Autonomous Resolution)**:
   > If you discover a $50,000 inventory forecasting error made by one of your 5 direct reports, **would you rather** give the subordinate 48 hours to quietly fix it before briefing your CEO—**OR** escalate the **latent risk** to your CEO immediately while the fix is still in progress?

## Activity 6: Pruning the Boardroom Memo ("An Irrelevant Sentence")
* **Adapted From**: *An Irrelevant Sentence* (Bolen, `split_053`)
* **Time**: 10 Minutes | **Skills**: Reading for Cohesion, Executive Register Recognition, Discourse Organization
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: In Bolen's `split_053`, the student reads short paragraphs where **one sentence violates the logic, cohesion, or register** of the text type. Here, YH audits **3 executive retail memos**. In each memo, one sentence either leaks into **trivial micromanagement**, **apologetic L1 hesitancy**, or **irrelevant operational noise**. YH must spot the sentence and explain *why* it undermines executive authority.

### Memo 1: Q3 APAC E-Commerce Restructuring (To: C-Suite Board)
> *(1) During Q3, my primary mandate was to reverse margin compression across our Southeast Asian marketplaces. (2) First, I spearheaded a comprehensive SKU rationalization, phasing out 120 low-margin items that were diluting our warehouse bandwidth. (3) I also personally checked the font size on the interns' Excel spreadsheets every Tuesday morning and told them which desk chairs to sit in. (4) As a direct result of focusing capital on our top 15 brand partnerships, net operating margin expanded by 8.4 percentage points.*
- **Irrelevant / Flawed Sentence**: **Sentence (3)** — Violates executive **span of control** by dropping from C-suite macro strategy into trivial **micromanagement**.

### Memo 2: Escalating a Supply Chain Bottleneck (To: VP of Operations)
> *(1) Looking at our November fulfillment projections, I want to flag a latent risk in our cold-chain logistics pipeline. (2) Our primary courier in Taipei is currently operating at 98% capacity, which risks delaying premium perishable deliveries by up to 36 hours. (3) I am so sorry to bother you with my bad English and I really hope you don't get angry at our team for this big problem. (4) To mitigate this proactively, we have pre-negotiated overflow capacity with a secondary carrier, contingent upon your sign-off by Thursday.*
- **Irrelevant / Flawed Sentence**: **Sentence (3)** — Drops into the **apologetic Beta Trap / L1 *Enryo* transfer**, undermining the crisp 70/30 proactive governance of Sentences (1), (2), and (4).

### Memo 3: Delegating Q4 Campaign Execution (To: 5 Regional Directors)
> *(1) As we enter the Q4 peak trading window, our overarching objective is to achieve 200% GMV growth while keeping Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) under $35. (2) Each Regional Director has full self-autonomy to allocate local promotional budgets across the highest-performing channels in your territory. (3) Yesterday I had fermented natto and raw egg for breakfast, which is a very unusual concept of breakfast in Europe. (4) We will convene on November 15 for a 30-minute half-goal circuit breaker to review conversion velocity and recalibrate underperforming ad sets.*
- **Irrelevant / Flawed Sentence**: **Sentence (3)** — Playful callback to YH & Louie's Oct 21 warm-up (`03:51–05:13`), testing rapid skimming and cohesion!

## Activity 8: High-Noise Crisis Call & Closed-Loop Readback ("Excuse Me, What Did You Say?")
* **Adapted From**: *Excuse Me, What Did You Say?* (Bolen, `split_046`)
* **Time**: 10 Minutes | **Skills**: Inferring Meaning Under Pressure, Tactical Clarification, Closed-Loop Readback
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: In `split_046`, the teacher delivers instructions quickly, with simulated line interference or missing words, forcing the student to infer context and ask sharp, targeted clarification questions rather than saying *"Pardon?"* or guessing blindly (which caused the bomb explosions on Oct 23 at lines `321`, `597`, and `1742` when *"don't press"* vs *"press"* or *"hold"* vs *"release"* got crossed!).

### Pre-Teach the 4 Executive Readback Formulas:
1. **Isolating the Variable**: *"I caught the target SKU, but the cutoff time broke up—did you say **14:00** or **16:00**?"*
2. **Action Confirmation (Preventing the Oct 23 'Press' Trap)**: *"Before I execute: are you asking me to **deploy the patch immediately**, or **stage it and hold** until your signal?"*
3. **Spelling / Homophone Verification (Preventing the Oct 23 'YOUR / YOU'RE' Trap)**: *"To avoid any ambiguity—are we referencing **Tier A (T-I-E-R)** or **Tear-Down**?"*
4. **Closed-Loop Summary**: *"Understood. So to confirm: I will halt Campaign B, reallocate $15k to Search, and report back at 17:00. Correct?"*

### 3 Live "Garbled Crisis Transmission" Drills (Louie Reads Fast / Drops Words in Brackets):
- **Transmission 1 (Flash Sale Server Overload)**:
  > *"YH, our Singapore checkout gateway is spiking at 99% CPU! Do NOT—I repeat, [garbled cough]—shut down the primary database. Instead, reroute [mumbled: fifty / fifteen] percent of traffic to the Tokyo backup node and release the queue when latency drops below [four / fourteen] hundred milliseconds!"*
  - **YH's Task**: Immediately fire back a **Closed-Loop Readback** verifying the 3 critical variables (1. Do NOT shut down primary DB; 2. Verify *15%* vs *50%*; 3. Verify *400ms* vs *1,400ms*).
- **Transmission 2 (Urgent Supplier Contract Amendment)**:
  > *"YH, legal just reviewed the exclusive beauty distribution deal. Hold off on signing Section [4B / 4D] until they revise the minimum order quantity from [30,000 / 13,000] units, otherwise we'll shoot ourselves in the foot on Q1 warehousing costs!"*
- **Transmission 3 (Multi-Module Bomb / System Defusal Callback)**:
  > *"Look at Module 3 in the bottom-right quadrant: if the serial number ends in an [odd / even] digit and the indicator is flashing [amber / crimson], press and hold the override switch until the countdown timer shows a [4 / 5] in any position!"*

## Activity 10: The \$1M APAC Expansion & Conditional Concession Lab ("If I Had a Million Dollars")
* **Adapted From**: *If I Had a Million Dollars* (Bolen, `split_028`)
* **Time**: 15 Minutes | **Skills**: Second & Mixed Conditionals in Business, Strategic Capital Allocation, Conditional Negotiation
* **Pedagogical Rationale**: Bolen uses *If I Had a Million Dollars* to teach hypothetical conditionals (*"If I had $1M, I would..."*). For an APAC executive, we elevate this into two high-stakes boardroom drills:
  1. **Part A (Boardroom Capital Allocation)**: *"If the Board allocated an additional **$1,000,000** to your APAC division tomorrow, how would you deploy it across inventory, headcount, and technology to generate a 3x return?"*
  2. **Part B (Conditional Concession Framing in Brand Negotiations)**: Using high-level conditional structures (*"If you were prepared to grant us APAC exclusivity, we would commit to..."*, *"Provided that you can meet us on margin, we would be in a position to..."*) so YH never "leaves money on the table."

### The 4 Executive Conditional Structures to Enforce:
1. **Second Conditional (Strategic Allocation)**: *"If we **had** an incremental $1M in growth capital, I **would allocate** 60% to high-velocity SKU inventory and 40% to regional fulfillment automation."*
2. **Inverted Formal Conditional (C1/C2 Boardroom Polish)**: *"Were we **to expand** our warehouse footprint in Malaysia, we **could cut** cross-border shipping latency by 48 hours."*
3. **Conditional Concession (Negotiation Trade-Off)**: *"Provided that your brand **is willing to guarantee** a 42% wholesale margin, we **would be prepared to feature** your product line on our homepage banner during 11.11."*
4. **Defensive Boundary Conditional**: *"Unless we **install** a half-goal circuit breaker at Month 3, we **run the risk of** diluting our Q4 ROI."*

### Prompt Set A: The 240% APAC Turnaround Story
- **Question 1 (Situation & Mandate)**: *What was the exact commercial state of the APAC e-commerce business when you took command, and what was your primary mandate?*
- **Question 2 (Task & Hypothesis)**: *How did you combine quantitative metrics and qualitative insights to diagnose why resources were being diluted?*
- **Question 3 (Decisive Action — Personal Agency)**: *What specific SKU and brand-partnership restructuring did **you personally spearhead**?*
- **Question 4 (Quantifiable Result)**: *What measurable year-on-year revenue expansion and operational efficiency did your actions deliver?*
- **Question 5 (Formative Learning)**: *Looking back, how did this turnaround shape your leadership philosophy regarding data-driven focus?*

### Prompt Set B: Scaling 100+ Staff & Overcoming Information Asymmetry

- **Question 4 (Result)**: *How did shifting to the 70/30 upward reporting model transform executive trust and team velocity?*
- **Question 5 (Learning)**: *What is your core principle today for balancing subordinate self-autonomy with risk governance?*

## Activity 12: Executive Register "Odd One Out" & Boardroom "2 Truths and a Lie"
* **Adapted From**: *Odd One Out* (Bolen, `split_107`) & *2 Truths and a Lie* (Bolen, `split_112`)
* **Time**: 8–10 Minutes (Ideal Warm-Up or Cool-Down) | **Skills**: Lexical Nuance, Cross-Examination Questioning

### Part 12A: Executive Register "Odd One Out" (Adapted from `split_107`)
*Rule: In each group of 4 terms, 3 belong to the same executive semantic/register category and **1 is the Odd One Out** (either because of meaning, opposite polarity, or *Wasei-Eigo* / elementary register). YH must identify the odd one out and explain why in a full sentence.*

1. `spearhead` · `orchestrate` · `champion` · `micromanage`
   - **Odd One Out**: **`micromanage`** (The first three are positive C1/C2 verbs for leading a strategic initiative at a macro level; *micromanage* is a negative behavior that suffocates subordinate autonomy).
2. `information asymmetry` · `misalignment` · `out of the loop` · `seamless synergy`
   - **Odd One Out**: **`seamless synergy`** (The first three describe communication bottlenecks and reporting gaps; *seamless synergy* describes perfect alignment).
3. `lodge a complaint` · `escalate a grievance` · `make a claim` · `flag a bottleneck`
   - **Odd One Out**: **`make a claim`** (*Wasei-Eigo* false friend! In English, *make a claim* refers to insurance payouts or asserting a legal right, whereas the other three mean raising a customer or operational issue).
4. `foster development` · `cultivate talent` · `empower subordinates` · `grow up staff`
   - **Odd One Out**: **`grow up staff`** (Grammatically and collocationally incorrect L1 transfer; *grow up* is intransitive for children maturing, whereas the other three are executive leadership collocations).
5. `leave money on the table` · `dilute bandwidth` · `shoot yourself in the foot` · `deliver on time and under budget`
   - **Odd One Out**: **`deliver on time and under budget`** (The first three are idioms for commercial/operational mistakes and inefficiency; the fourth is a hallmark of project execution excellence).

### Part 12B: Boardroom "2 Truths and a Lie" (Adapted from `split_112`)
* **Procedure**: Both YH and Louie state **3 executive/commercial statements** about their real career, retail data, or leadership experiences (2 true, 1 fabricated).
* **The Cross-Examination Rule (from `split_112`)**: Before guessing the lie, the listener has **2 minutes of rapid-fire cross-examination** using skeptical executive probes:
  - *"Walk me through the quantitative breakdown of that—how did you calculate that margin?"*
  - *"Wait—from an operational standpoint, how was that feasible given your team's bandwidth?"*
  - *"If that were true, wouldn't it have cannibalized your existing product line?"*
