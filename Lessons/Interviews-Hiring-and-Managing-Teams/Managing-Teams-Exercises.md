## Exercise 1: Directive & Delegation Syntax (`Verb + Object + Infinitive or Gerund`)
*Source: English File Advanced 6A Grammar (TG p. 177 / SB p. 152)*  
**Context:** As an executive managing 5 regional directors across 100+ staff, how you frame instructions determines whether you sound like a micromanager or an authoritative leader.

### Grammar Rule Quick-Check:
- **`Verb + person + TO DO`** *(authoritative expectations & coaching)*: `expect`, `empower`, `encourage`, `urge`, `advise`, `remind`, `persuade`, `require`
- **`Verb + person + DO` (no *to*)** *(granting autonomy or enforcing rules)*: `let`, `make`, `help`
- **`Verb + person + (from) + -ING`** *(risk control & process governance)*: `prevent (from)`, `stop (from)`, `involve`, `keep (from)`

### Task: Rewrite each management statement using the bolded verb in brackets:

1. *"I told my regional directors: 'Do not wait until the end of the quarter to report bad news.'"* (**expect**)  
   ➔ `I expect my regional directors ___________________________________________________`

2. *"I give my 5 regional heads full freedom to adjust local store promotions."* (**let** / **empower**)  
   ➔ `I let my regional heads ___________________ / I empower them ___________________`

3. *"Our half-goal circuit breaker makes sure merchandising teams don't burn capital on slow-moving SKUs."* (**prevent**)  
   ➔ `Our half-goal circuit breaker prevents merchandising teams _______________________`

4. *"I strongly pushed our Taiwan country manager to reallocate 30% of his staff to high-margin brands."* (**urge**)  
   ➔ `I urged our Taiwan country manager ______________________________________________`

5. *"Don't forget to submit the Q4 inventory forecast before Friday's board sync."* (**remind**)  
   ➔ `I reminded the team lead ________________________________________________________`

6. *"Managing a 100-person retail organization means my directors must take ownership of store-level hiring."* (**involve**)  
   ➔ `Managing a 100-person organization involves my directors _________________________`

7. *"When a campaign fails to hit 50% of its target, I force the team to pause and audit the data."* (**make**)  
   ➔ `When a campaign misses the 50% mark, I make the team _____________________________`

8. *"I convinced the warehouse supervisor to adopt the new automated SKU tracking workflow."* (**persuade**)  
   ➔ `I persuaded the warehouse supervisor ____________________________________________`

## Exercise 2: Expressing Urgency & Setting Standards (`It's high time` / `I'd rather` / `Supposed to`)
*Source: English File Advanced 5B Grammar: Unreal Uses of Past Tenses (TG p. 176) & 7A Grammar: Permission, Obligation & Necessity (TG p. 179)*  
**Context:** When a team is slipping behind schedule or ignoring standard operating procedures (SOPs), executives use **unreal past tenses** (*It's high time we revamped...*, *I'd rather you escalated...*) and **obligation structures** (*supposed to / meant to / required to*) to assert firm control without shouting.

### Task: Complete or transform each sentence for a Regional Management Team Sync:

1. **Enforcing a missed milestone (`supposed to` + base verb):**  
   *"The Singapore team only onboarded 12 brands this month, even though the KPI was 25."*  
   ➔ *"The Singapore team **was supposed to** `________________________________________`."*

2. **Calling for overdue action (`It's high time we/you` + past simple verb):**  
   *"We are still using manual Excel sheets for regional inventory, and it's slowing us down."*  
   ➔ *"It's high time we **`____________________`** (automate) our regional inventory reporting."*

3. **Redirecting a subordinate's priority (`I'd rather you` + past simple verb):**  
   *"Please stop spending time on low-margin clearance items and focus on our top 10 brands."*  
   ➔ *"I'd rather you **`____________________`** (focus) your team's bandwidth on our top 10 brands rather than low-yield clearance items."*

4. **Correcting an escalation failure (`I'd rather you had` + past participle):**  
   *"You waited until Week 6 to tell me the supplier was delayed instead of flagging it in Week 2."*  
   ➔ *"I'd rather you **`____________________`** (flag) the supplier bottleneck at the 50% mark instead of waiting until Week 6."*

5. **Setting non-negotiable governance (`required to` vs. `permitted to`):**  
   *"Store managers can offer a 10% VIP discount, but any discount above 20% needs Regional Director sign-off."*  
   ➔ *"Store managers are **`____________________`** offer a 10% discount, but they are **`____________________`** obtain Regional Director sign-off for anything above 20%."*

- **The Situation:** Two of your top Regional Directors—**Alex (E-Commerce Director)** and **lina (Physical Retail Stores Director)**—are clashing fiercely in your weekly management meeting:
  - **Alex** insists that 80% of Q4 limited-edition stock should be allocated to the **online store** because e-commerce grew 240% YoY and has higher margins.
  - **Lina** is furious, arguing that starving physical flagship stores of flagship stock is killing foot traffic and demoralizing her 60 store staff.
- **Your Task as Executive Chair (5 Minutes):**
  1. Open and chair the meeting decisively (*Osborne's Thomas Chairing Playbook, p. 144*).
  2. Acknowledge both perspectives using **1A Half-Agreeing** (*"I see what you mean about online velocity, Alex; however, Lina has a valid point regarding flagship store morale..."*).
  3. Propose a data-driven **compromise / omnichannel trade-off** and set a **half-goal circuit breaker** at Week 2 of Q4 to reallocate inventory dynamically based on real-time sell-through.
