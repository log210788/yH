### Exercise 4: Managerial Directives & Upward Linkers (*English File 6A TG p. 177 & 1B TG p. 168*)

#### Part A: Directive Syntax (`Verb + Object + Infinitive or Gerund`)
1. *"I expect my regional directors **to escalate** store bottlenecks before the 50% mark."* (`expect + sb + to-infinitive`)
2. *"I let my team **make** local merchandising decisions / I empower my team **to make** local merchandising decisions."* (`let + sb + bare infinitive` vs. `empower + sb + to-infinitive`)
3. *"Our half-goal circuit breaker prevents junior buyers **from wasting** budget on low-margin brands."* (`prevent + sb + from + -ing`)
4. *"I urged the CEO **to review** the latent supply-chain risks before Q4."* (`urge + sb + to-infinitive`)
5. *"Scaling an organization of 100+ staff involves regional leads **taking** ownership of hiring."* (`involve + sb + -ing`)

#### Part B: Executive Case Narrative — Linkers (*1B TG p. 168*)
1. **Nevertheless** (or **However**)
2. **Owing to**
3. **So as not to**
4. **so as to**
5. **Despite**
6. **Consequently**

## 🎭 Facilitation Notes for Section 3 (Live Executive Simulations)

### Simulation 1: "Have I Got the Job?" (*1B Communicative, TG p. 194*)
- **In Round A (YH as Interviewer):** Play a candidate who sounds enthusiastic on the surface but gives slightly vague, "we"-heavy answers (*"In my last retail job, we did a lot of promotions and sales went up"*). Force YH to probe deeper:
  - *Target probes from YH:* *"What was your specific personal mandate in that campaign?"*, *"Tell me about a time when a promotion underperformed at the 50% mark—how did you handle it?"*
- **In Round B (YH as Candidate):** Run Osborne's **"Roger" Zero-Latency Rule** (*Ch. 5, p. 100*) on Question 1: YH must initiate his response within **1.5 seconds** using an executive framing opener (*"At the core of my governance model for a 100-person organization is..."*).

### Simulation 2: Executive Time & Delegation Audit (*5A Communicative, TG p. 201*)
- Use Osborne's **Devil's Advocate technique (*Ch. 5, p. 101*)**:
  - When YH discusses Statement 1 or 5, challenge him: *"Isn't it faster to just step in and fix the SKU spreadsheet yourself rather than waiting for a junior manager to figure it out?"*
  - Require YH to defend his high-trust delegation philosophy using **Cleft Sentences** (*"What happens when an executive micromanages is..."*) and **6A Directive Syntax** (*"I'd rather empower my directors to solve it than create a bottleneck around myself"*).

### Simulation 3: 1-on-1 Enactment with "Marcus" (*Osborne Ch. 7, p. 143 + English File 1A*)
- **Your Role as Marcus:** Be defensive at first:
  - *Line 1:* *"YH, I know we're only at 35% of the SKU onboarding target in Week 5, but the team has been snowed under and I didn't want to bother you until I had good news."*
  - *Line 2:* *"If we just push the launch date back by two weeks, I think we can catch up without cutting any brands."*
- **What to Listen for from YH:**
  1. **Polite Disagreement (*English File 1A*):** *"I appreciate that the team has been pressed for time, Marcus; however, hiding a bottleneck until Week 5 creates severe information asymmetry."*
  2. **The 70/30 & Circuit-Breaker Principle:** Explaining that escalating at 50% isn't "admitting failure"—it's protecting company capital so leadership can reallocate resources early.
  3. **Actionable Close (*Osborne p. 147*):** Ending the 1-on-1 with a clear timeline and mitigation plan.
