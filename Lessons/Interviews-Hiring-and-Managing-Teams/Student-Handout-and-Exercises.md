## 1. The 30-Term Executive Upgrade Matrix (Hiring, Interviews & Managing Teams)

> **Core Rule**: In our hiring interviews and team management enactments, elementary conversational phrases (*grow up, bad things happen, useless things, good worker, fire, boss, check every small thing*) are **off-limits**. Use this C1 matrix to project authoritative executive presence.

### A. Interviews, Executive Ownership & Career Trajectory (*English File 1B* + *STAR-L*)
| Basic Phrase (Avoid) | C1 Executive Upgrade | How to Use It in a Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "We did / We analyzed..." | **I spearheaded / My mandate was to...** | *"In Q3, **my primary mandate was to** turnaround our APAC e-commerce margins."* |
| Moving up fast in a company | **On the fast track / Climb the career ladder** | *"High-performing store managers are put **on the fast track** to regional director roles."* |
| Fired because the company cut jobs | **Made redundant / Laid off (Downsizing)** | *"Following the restructuring, 15 back-office roles were **made redundant**."* |
| Fired for bad behavior/performance | **Dismissed / Terminated (Sacked)** | *"He was **dismissed** after repeatedly violating our vendor compliance policy."* |
| Extra non-salary benefits | **Perks / Incentives / Compensation package** | *"Beyond base salary, our **compensation package** includes performance-tied equity **perks**."* |
| Temporary 1-year contract | **Fixed-term contract** | *"We initially hire regional merchandising consultants on a 12-month **fixed-term contract**."* |
| Boring, repetitive job with no future | **Monotonous / Dead-end role** | *"If talented staff feel trapped in a **monotonous, dead-end role**, turnover spikes."* |
| High-pressure, influential job | **High-powered / Demanding position** | *"Leading a 100-person retail organization is a **high-powered, demanding** executive role."* |
| Leave for family / personal crisis | **Compassionate leave / Parental leave** | *"We granted the store manager two weeks of **compassionate leave**."* |
| Main skills I am best at | **Core competencies / Track record** | *"My **core competencies** lie in omnichannel scaling and P&L turnaround."* |

### B. Appraising Talent & Describing Personality (*English File 1A* + *Polishes*)
| Basic Phrase (Avoid) | C1 Executive Upgrade | How to Use It in a Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "See my subordinates grow up" | **Foster professional development / Cultivate talent** | *"My philosophy centers on **fostering my subordinates' professional development**."* |
| Good at finding solutions alone | **Resourceful / Self-sufficient** | *"I need **resourceful, self-sufficient** regional directors who don't need hand-holding."* |
| Careful and does every duty well | **Conscientious / Thorough** | *"She is a **conscientious** auditor who is **thorough** with inventory data."* |
| Practical and realistic | **Down-to-earth / Pragmatic** | *"We need a **pragmatic, down-to-earth** operations lead rather than a pure theorist."* |
| Honest and direct | **Straightforward** | *"I appreciate **straightforward** upward reporting when store KPIs are slipping."* |
| Reliable and calm under pressure | **Steady / Composed** | *"During peak Q4 holiday traffic, he remains a **steady** hand at the helm."* |
| Too easy on subordinates | **A soft touch** | *"If a regional manager is **a soft touch**, store compliance standards quickly erode."* |
| Difficult / annoying colleague | **A pain in the neck / Uncooperative** | *"To be diplomatic, that vendor can be **a bit of a challenge** to align with."* |
| Cold, unemotional manager | **Aloof / A cold fish** | *"In interviews, he **came across as** somewhat **aloof** rather than collaborative."* |
| Trust my gut instinct on a hire | **Go with my gut feeling** | *"The data looked fine, but I **went with my gut feeling** that the cultural fit wasn't right."* |

### C. Managing Teams, Delegation & Risk Governance (*English File 5A, 6A, 7A* + *Osborne Ch. 7*)
| Basic Phrase (Avoid) | C1 Executive Upgrade | How to Use It in a Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tell every small step to staff | **Micromanage (vs. Empower with autonomy)** | *"I set the macro vision and **empower my directors with autonomy** rather than **micromanaging**."* |
| "Split resources to useless things" | **Dilute operational bandwidth across low-yield tasks** | *"We cannot **dilute our operational bandwidth** across low-margin SKUs."* |
| "Once bad things happen, report" | **Escalate operational bottlenecks with mitigation options** | *"When a **bottleneck** emerges, **escalate it immediately** alongside viable **mitigation options**."* |
| Boss knows less than the team | **Information asymmetry / Upward management** | *"To bridge **information asymmetry**, I devote 30% of my briefing to latent risks."* |
| Stop point if 50% target fails | **Half-goal circuit breaker / Milestone check** | *"I install a **half-goal circuit breaker** so underperforming campaigns don't burn capital."* |
| Very busy / Not enough time | **Pressed for time / Snowed under** | *"Our merchandising team is **pressed for time** ahead of the Double-11 launch."* |
| Late with work | **Get behind with work / Slip behind schedule** | *"Two regional stores are **getting behind with** their seasonal inventory audits."* |
| Not enough staff vs. doing better | **Understaffed / Outperform** | *"Despite being **understaffed** in Q2, the Singapore team **outperformed** their revenue target."* |
| Bureaucracy / Too many rules | **Red tape** | *"I streamlined our sign-off workflow to cut through unnecessary **red tape**."* |
| Focus on the main strategy | **Focus on the big picture / Get to the heart of the problem** | *"My role as APAC lead is to **focus on the big picture** and **get to the heart of the problem**."* |

#### Part B: The Executive Softening Drill (*English File 1A TG p. 15*)
> **Teacher's Guide Rule (TG p. 15):** In international executive interviews and appraisals, native speakers rarely use blunt negative adjectives (*"He is lazy / aggressive / stubborn"*). Instead, they soften criticism using:
> - **`can be a bit / somewhat + [negative adjective]`**
> - **`tends to be a bit of a + [negative noun/idiom]`**
> - **`isn't always as [positive adjective] as we'd like`**

*Rewrite these 3 blunt criticisms so they sound measured, diplomatic, and boardroom-ready:*
1. **Blunt:** *"Director D is too weak and never disciplines his staff."*  
   ➔ **Diplomatic:** *"Director D `_________________________________________________________`."*
2. **Blunt:** *"This candidate is cold and unfriendly in team meetings."*  
   ➔ **Diplomatic:** *"In cross-functional syncs, this candidate `_____________________________`."*
3. **Blunt:** *"Director E is a micromanager who doesn't trust her subordinates."*  
   ➔ **Diplomatic:** *"Director E `_________________________________________________________`."*

### Simulation 3: 1-on-1 Enactment — Enforcing the "Half-Goal Circuit Breaker" (*Osborne Ch. 7, p. 143 + English File 1A Diplomatic Disagreement*)
- **The Scenario:** You delegated a major Q4 brand-onboarding campaign to **Marcus**, one of your 5 Regional Directors. You gave him high-trust autonomy, with one clear rule: if the campaign hadn't hit 50% of its SKU onboarding target by Week 3, he had to trigger the **circuit breaker** and escalate to you. It is now Week 5: the campaign is at 35%, and Marcus hid the delay hoping his team could "catch up quietly."
- **Your Mission (6 Minutes):**
  1. Open the 1-on-1 calmly and state the agenda (*Osborne Chairing Playbook, p. 144*).
  2. Address the **information asymmetry** and explain *why* hiding a bottleneck violates your high-trust autonomy model.
  3. When Marcus makes excuses (*"We were understaffed and I didn't want to bother you with bad news"*), use **Half-Agreeing & Politely Disagreeing** (*"I appreciate that your team was pressed for time; however..."*, *"I agree up to a point, but..."*).
  4. Agree on concrete **mitigation options** and set expectations using **6A Directive Syntax** (*"Going forward, I expect you to..."*, *"This circuit breaker is designed to prevent us from..."*).
