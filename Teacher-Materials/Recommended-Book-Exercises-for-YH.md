## Part 1: High-Impact 1-to-1 Exercises from *Teaching English One to One* (Priscilla Osborne)

Osborne's methodology is tailor-made for senior 1-to-1 business learners like YH. Below are the highest-yield exercises and frameworks mapped directly to YH's diagnostic needs (from [`Learning needs.txt`](file:///d:/Documents/yH/messages/Learning%20needs.txt), [`LESSON_NOTES_TODAY.md`](file:///d:/Documents/yH/LESSON_NOTES_TODAY.md), and [`polishes.json`](file:///d:/Documents/yH/polishes.json)):

### 1. Upgrading B1/B2 Speech to C1 Executive Vernacular ("We put more items..." $\rightarrow$ "I onboarded high-margin SKUs...")
- **3-Tier Reformulation & 100-Word Transcript Co-Editing** (*Ch. 6, pp. 118–121, Case Study: Martin*)
  - **Why for YH:** In his Oct 21 lesson, YH used elementary phrasing like *"useless things"*, *"bad things happen"*, and *"subordinates are grown up"*. Osborne warns that simple grammar correction creates stilted English; instead, use a 3-column layout:
    1. **Original (YH's B1/B2 output):** *"Once some bad things happen, you should report immediately."*
    2. **Grammatical Fix:** *"Once something bad happens, you should report it immediately."*
    3. **Idiomatic/Executive Reformulation (C1):** *"The moment an operational bottleneck emerges, escalate it immediately with viable mitigation options."*
  - **Exercise:** Take a 100-word verbatim chunk from YH's [`lesson -starl.txt`](file:///d:/Documents/yH/transcripts/lesson%20-starl.txt) transcript and have him co-edit it down to a punchy 30-word executive statement (the **KISS Principle**, *Ch. 7, p. 136*).
- **"Job Talk" + Narrative Language Summary & 3-Word Skeleton Reconstruction** (*Ch. 5, pp. 89–92, Case Studies: Axel & Peter; Ch. 7, p. 156*)
  - **Exercise:** Have YH spend 5–8 minutes explaining an actual APAC e-commerce mechanism (e.g., how he isolates top-selling brands or sets his "half-goal circuit breaker" for regional directors). Instead of giving him a word list, write his explanation up as a **polished C1 narrative paragraph** (*p. 91*). Then test him using **Key-Word Skeleton Prompts** (*p. 92*, e.g., `dilute – bandwidth – low-yield` or `asymmetry – escalate – mitigation`) where he must reconstruct the executive sentence orally.
- **The 12-Exercise Master Recycling Template & Oral Error-Sheet Rapid Fire** (*Ch. 5, pp. 93–95; Ch. 6, pp. 123–129*)
  - **Exercise:** Start each lesson with a 5-minute **Oral Error-Sheet Drill** (*p. 126*): read aloud the *"You said..."* column from [`polishes.json`](file:///d:/Documents/yH/polishes.json) and have YH rapidly fire back the C1 *"You should have said..."* version. Follow with **Metaphorical Extension Questions** (*pp. 94–95*) and **Exercise 10: Fine Distinctions** (*p. 128*, e.g., contrasting *grow up* [children] vs. *foster professional development / cultivate talent* [subordinates]).

### 2. Overcoming Japanese L1 Transfer, Mora-Timing & Hesitancy
- **"Japanese-English" vs. "English-English" Awareness** (*Ch. 6, p. 115, Case Study: Yoshi; Exercise 7: Rewriting, p. 128*)
  - **Why for YH:** Osborne specifically highlights a Japanese learner (*Yoshi*) who translates topic-heavy, noun-heavy Japanese structures directly into English.
  - **Exercise:** Use **Exercise 7 (p. 128)** to drill converting passive/collective `"we"` + nominalized structures into active executive verbs (`We had a 240% expansion` $\rightarrow$ `I spearheaded the audit and delivered 240% YoY revenue expansion`).
- **Visual Flashcard / Board Cross Interrupts for Fossilised Habits** (*Ch. 6, p. 121*)
  - **Exercise:** During YH's STAR-L or PREP deliveries, do not interrupt his flow verbally. Instead, hold up a visual cue card (or mark an `X` on screen) whenever he uses collective `"we"` instead of `"I"` for personal leadership mandates, drops a Katakana loanword (*claim, cospa, remake*), or uses a Tier-1 word (*good, bad, many, thing*).
- **"Marking Up" a Script for Sense Groups, Liaison & Prominence** (*Ch. 5, pp. 104–107; Ch. 6, p. 125*)
  - **Why for YH:** Directly counters Japanese mora-timing (giving every syllable equal length) and vowel epenthesis.
  - **Exercise:** Take YH's 90-second STAR-L script and have him mark it up across 3 layers before recording:
    1. **Sense Groups (`/` and `//`):** Mark micro-pauses (`/`) and full sentence pitch-drops (`//`, *p. 125*).
    2. **Liaison Loops (`_`):** Draw linking bridges between terminal consonants and following vowels (*p. 107*) so he links words smoothly instead of inserting trailing `/u/` or `/o/` vowels.
    3. **Prominence (CAPS/Underlines):** Underline the 1–2 nuclear-stressed content words per chunk (especially metrics like `240%` and active verbs like `SPEAR-headed`), forcing weak schwa `/ə/` reductions on function words (*p. 106*).
- **The "Roger" Zero-Latency Response Drill** (*Ch. 5, p. 100, Case Study: Roger*)
  - **Why for YH:** Eliminates hesitation before speaking in meetings.
  - **Exercise:** Fire 6 rapid-fire boardroom questions at YH. **Grade ONLY response latency (<1.5 seconds to start speaking) and speech rate**—zero grammar or vocabulary corrections during the drill—training him to launch immediately with an executive framing buffer (*"From a commercial standpoint..."*, *"Looking at our Q3 trajectory..."*).

### 3. Real-World Business Enactments Across YH's 7 Domains (Chapter 7 & 13)
- **The 50% Enactment / 30% Feedback Loop** (*Ch. 1, p. 15; Ch. 7, pp. 138, 143*)
  - Replace hypothetical role-plays with **Enactments** (rehearsing YH's actual past or upcoming work situations). Spend 10–15% having YH brief you on the counterpart (e.g., his CEO or one of his 4–5 regional directors), 50% running the enactment, and 25–30% on business + linguistic reformulation.
- **Negotiations: Supermarket Trading Terms & M-D-I Matrix** (*Ch. 7, pp. 144–146, Example 1: Supermarket Chain*)
  - **Exact Match for YH's E-Commerce/Retail Role:** Page 146 features a retail negotiation over **payment terms, minimum order quantities (MOQs), promotional subsidies, delivery points, and new SKU listings**. Have YH map his **Minimum / Desirable / Ideal (M-D-I)** objectives (*p. 145*) and enact the 7 negotiation stages while replacing blunt phrases with cooperative conditional framing.
- **Presentations: The "Miroslaw" Hostile Q&A Pressure-Test** (*Ch. 7, p. 141, Case Study: Miroslaw*)
  - Have YH share a real APAC retail KPI slide or budget forecast. Play a skeptical board member who interrupts with rapid, challenging questions (*"Why shouldn't we cut the tail-end SKUs entirely?"*, *"How do you justify this CAC?"*) to pressure-test his **NMTA** and **Objection Aikido** skills.
- **Managing Teams & Upward Management: The "Yves" Conflict Enactment & "Jean Schmidt" Accountability Email** (*Ch. 7, pp. 143, 152*)
  - Enact a 1-on-1 where YH enforces his **"half-goal circuit breaker"** with a regional director who failed to escalate a problem early, or adapt the **Monthly Report deadline email** (*p. 152*) into an upward/downward management writing drill.
- **Audio-Only Escalation Calls & Diplomatic Boundary Role Cards** (*Ch. 7, pp. 148–150, 154–155*)
  - **Camera-Off Phone Enactments (*Oscar*, p. 148 & *Felco Fabrics vs. Turbo Clothing*, pp. 154–155):** Run a camera-off call resolving a defective merchandise claim (*Felco/Turbo*) or addressing a manager who bypassed channels (*Oscar*). Turning cameras off forces razor-sharp pronunciation and verbal signposting (great preparation alongside his *Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes* spatial communication training!).
  - **Role Cards E & F (*p. 150*):** Practice polite, non-apologetic pushback when an overseas HQ colleague wants to extend an exhausting meeting past 18:45.
- **Omnichannel Retail & Competitor Website Audit** (*Ch. 13, pp. 236–238, Supermarkets & Sara's Retail Survey*)
  - Adapt the retail comparison project (*pp. 236–238*) by having YH screen-share an e-commerce store walk comparing his platform against a competitor on assortment, pricing architecture, and UX.

## Part 2: Specific Units & Photocopiable Worksheets from *English File 4th Edition Advanced — Teacher's Guide*

The *English File Advanced (C1) Teacher's Guide* includes **over 65 pages of ready-to-use Photocopiable Grammar, Communicative, and Vocabulary worksheets** (TG pp. 167–231) plus lesson guidance that map directly to YH's needs:

### 1. Best Main Units & Exercises for YH
1. **Unit 5B: *Not for profit?* (TG pp. 82–87 | SB pp. 50–53 & p. 122) — E-Commerce, Pitching & Proposals**
   - **5 Listening (TG pp. 85–86):** Authentic interview with the co-founder of an e-commerce brand (*Pasta Evangelists*) covering supplier sourcing, seed capital, angel investors, and scaling.
   - **6 Speaking (TG p. 86):** Structured **Business Proposal Pitch** (product, target market, pricing, marketing, financial backing).
   - **7 Writing (TG p. 87 / SB p. 122):** Writing a formal **Executive Business Proposal** (headings, pros/cons, generalizing, and making recommendations).
   - **1 Vocabulary (TG pp. 82–83 / SB p. 168):** Financial & commercial lexis and idioms (*in the red/black, tighten your belt, quote, budget, shares*).
   - **4 Grammar (TG pp. 84–85):** Unreal past tenses (*It's high time we..., I'd rather you...*) for diplomatic urgency with subordinates or vendors.
2. **Unit 1B: *A job for life?* (TG pp. 19–25 | SB pp. 10–13) — Work Lexis, Rhythm of English & Formal Linkers**
   - **1 Vocabulary (TG pp. 19–20 / SB p. 163):** High-level work collocations (*fast track, career ladder, high-powered, demanding, perks, laid off vs. made redundant, staff vs. workforce*).
   - **3 Pronunciation (TG p. 22):** **The Rhythm of English** — explicit stress-timing and weak-form training on workplace opinions (directly targets YH's Japanese mora-timing).
   - **5 Grammar (TG p. 23):** **Discourse Markers (1): Linkers** of reason, result, purpose, and contrast (*consequently, owing to, so as to, nevertheless*) with formal vs. spoken register contrast.
3. **Unit 1A: *We are family* (TG pp. 12–18 | SB pp. 6–9) — Diplomatic Disagreement & Appraising Staff**
   - **2 Speaking (TG pp. 13–14):** **Half-agreeing and politely disagreeing** (*"I see what you mean, but..."*, *"I agree up to a point, but..."*, *"I agree in theory, but..."*) with intonation modeling.
   - **4 Vocabulary (TG pp. 15–16 / SB p. 162):** Evaluating personality & work style (*conscientious, resourceful, thorough, straightforward, self-sufficient*) + **TG Softening Note (p. 15)** on how to soften negative feedback diplomatically in performance reviews.
4. **Unit 5A: *No time for anything* (TG pp. 75–81 | SB pp. 46–49) — Executive Hedging, Time & Linking**
   - **4 Grammar (TG p. 77):** **Distancing** (*It appears that..., There are thought to be..., apparently, according to*) for cautious boardroom reporting and negotiations.
   - **6 Vocabulary & 7 Pronunciation (TG pp. 79–80):** **Expressions with time** (*pressed for time, for the time being, make up for lost time*) + **Linking in short phrases** (connected speech).
5. **Unit 6A: *Help, I need somebody!* (TG pp. 92–95 | SB pp. 56–59) — Presentations, Q&A & Delegation Syntax**
   - **2 Grammar (TG p. 93):** **Verb + object + infinitive/gerund** (*expect/encourage/urge/advise sb to do sth*, *involve/prevent sb from doing sth*) — core syntax for delegating to regional directors.
   - **4 Listening & 5 Speaking (TG pp. 93–94):** Summarizing a talk's thesis in two sentences + **Giving a well-structured presentation and taking questions**.
   - **6 Vocabulary & Pronunciation (TG pp. 94–95):** Compound adjectives (*long-term, low-cost, groundbreaking, labour-saving*) and primary/secondary stress.
6. **Unit 3A & 3B (TG pp. 45–55) — Corporate Conflict Metaphors, `get` Idioms & Meeting Signposts**
   - **3A Vocabulary (TG pp. 48–49 / SB p. 164):** Idioms & phrasal verbs with `get` (*get to the bottom of, get your act together, get the wrong end of the stick, get through to*).
   - **3B Vocabulary (TG p. 51 / SB p. 165):** **Metaphorical uses of conflict verbs** in business (*defend a policy, shoot down an idea, drop a bombshell, trigger a price war, allies*).
   - **3B Grammar (TG p. 55):** **Discourse Markers (2)** for steering meetings (*incidentally, as a matter of fact, in any case, on the whole, all in all, besides*).
7. **Unit 4A (TG pp. 62–63) & Unit 10B (TG p. 160) — High-Impact Rhetorical Emphasis**
   - **4A Inversion:** *Not only did we..., Under no circumstances should...*
   - **10B Cleft Sentences & Intonation:** *What we really need to focus on is...*, *The main reason why... is...* — instant upgrade for YH's **PREP**, **STAR-L**, and **Pitch Anything** hooks.
8. **Targeted L1 Japanese Pronunciation Drills Across Units:**
   - **1B (TG p. 22):** Stress-timed sentence rhythm.
   - **3A (TG p. 46):** Identifying speaker attitudes via pitch/intonation contours.
   - **3B (TG p. 52):** Shifting stress in word families (*execute / execution*).
   - **6B (TG p. 97):** Minimal pairs `/æ/` vs. `/ʌ/` (which merge into Japanese `/a/`).
   - **8A (TG p. 125):** The weak schwa `/ə/` sound.
   - **9B (TG p. 143):** **Words with silent syllables** (*business, comfortable, different, temperature*) — critical for purging Katakana vowel insertion, paired with **9B Ex. 3 (TG p. 144): Relaying precise instructions** and **9B Ex. 7 (TG p. 147): Writing a formal escalation/complaint email**.
