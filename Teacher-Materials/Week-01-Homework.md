## Part 1: The 3-Second Cognitive Reflex Sprint — "Define What It Is" (Audio Memo)
*In class, we trained your mouth to start speaking within 3 seconds without saying "Ano..." or stalling.*
- **Instructions**: Choose at least **4 to 6 triggers** from the bank below (or challenge yourself to do all 10!). Give yourself **3 seconds** to begin speaking, and record a **20-to-30-second continuous voice note** for each prompt on your phone or in our online Recording Studio:
  1. **Trigger 1: "A Bottleneck"** ➔ Define what it is: describe a process in your office, project, or daily life where everything slows down.
  2. **Trigger 2: "Bandwidth"** ➔ Define what it is: explain when you or a colleague have "limited bandwidth" vs. "free bandwidth".
  3. **Trigger 3: "A Trade-off"** ➔ Define what it is: give an everyday or work example where choosing one benefit means sacrificing another (e.g. speed vs. quality).
  4. **Trigger 4: "A Workaround"** ➔ Define what it is: describe a situation where the official software or tool failed, and what temporary fix was used.
  5. **Trigger 5: "Out of the Loop vs. In the Loop"** ➔ Contrast how it feels when colleagues forget to update you vs. when you are kept informed.
  6. **Trigger 6: "Scope Creep"** ➔ Describe a project or task that started small and gradually expanded out of control.
  7. **Trigger 7: "A Blind Spot"** ➔ Define what it is: describe a situation where a team or person missed an obvious flaw because they were too close to the work.
  8. **Trigger 8: "A Handoff"** ➔ Contrast a smooth, seamless project transfer with a messy, disorganized handoff.
  9. **Trigger 9: "Micromanagement"** ➔ Describe how excessive monitoring affects employee motivation and confidence.
  10. **Trigger 10: "The Downside (vs. Upside)"** ➔ Compare the negative risks of a major proposal against its positive potential.

#### Pro Tip: The 3-Angle Definition Formula
*When defining on the fly, immediately anchor your sentence with one of these three variations:*
- **Angle A (Functional Meaning)**: *"It essentially refers to a situation where..."*
- **Angle B (Direct Contrast)**: *"It's practically the polar opposite of [X]—instead of [Y], it involves..."*
- **Angle C (Real-World Example)**: *"For example, imagine you are at work and... that's a classic case of it."*

## Part 4: Pronunciation & Word Stress Self-Audit
*Read each sentence aloud into your microphone or phone voice memo. Ensure you observe the 2-Syllable Stress Shift (Nouns = 1st syllable; Verbs = 2nd syllable) and chop trailing vowels on words ending in consonants.*

1. **PROJ-ect** (Noun) vs. **pro-JECT** (Verb):  
   *"We need to pro-JECT our quarterly expenses before approving this multi-million dollar PROJ-ect."*
2. **REC-ord** (Noun) vs. **re-CORD** (Verb):  
   *"The team has a proven track REC-ord, and we plan to re-CORD our highest quarterly profits this year."*
3. **PRES-ent** (Noun/Adj) vs. **pre-SENT** (Verb):  
   *"At the PRES-ent moment, our priority is to pre-SENT our findings to the executive board."*
4. **Consonant Chopping Drill (No /u/ or /o/ endings!)**:  
   *"The key clien**t** assessed the ris**k** and provided constructive feedbac**k** on the contrac**t**."*
