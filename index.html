Drill 2: Navigating Workplace Responsibility & Subtext

Read each sentence out loud by punching ONLY the stressed word. Then click "Reveal Subtext" to inspect what the listener actually hears!

"I didn't tell him to cancel the project."
Stress on: Subject (I)
"Someone else gave that order—don't look at me or hold me accountable!"
"I DIDN'T tell him to cancel the project."
Stress on: Auxiliary (DIDN'T)
"Strong, categorical denial of a direct accusation."
"I didn't TELL him to cancel the project."
Stress on: Verb (TELL)
"I might have hinted or implied it, but I never officially authorized or ordered it."
"I didn't tell HIM to cancel the project."
Stress on: Object (HIM)
"I spoke to the team lead or the engineering director, not to him!"
"I didn't tell him to CANCEL the project."
Stress on: Action (CANCEL)
"I suggested pausing or deprioritizing it, not killing it completely!"
"I didn't tell him to cancel the PROJECT."
Stress on: Noun (PROJECT)
"I said cancel today's sprint planning meeting, not the entire initiative!"
Drill 3: Two-Syllable Business Word Stress (Noun vs. Verb)

English rule: Nouns usually take stress on the 1st syllable; Verbs take stress on the 2nd syllable.

Word Pair Noun (1st Syllable Stress) Verb (2nd Syllable Stress)
RECORD "Break the REC-ord." "Can you re-CORD this demo?"
PRESENT "It's a birthday PRES-ent." "Please pre-SENT the findings."
PROJECT "Who owns this PROJ-ect?" "We pro-JECT 20% growth."
CONTRACT "Sign the CON-tract." "Metals con-TRACT in cold."
PROGRESS "We made great PROG-ress." "How will the task pro-GRESS?"
