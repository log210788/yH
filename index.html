Hi YH. In our first session, we focused on breaking the habit of relying on elementary conversational default verbs (Tier 1) and transitioning decisively to the language used by engineering directors, project leads, and department heads in multinational organizations (Tier 3).

Using words like do, make, big problem, or talk about is grammatically functional, but it conceals your seniority and technical expertise. This worksheet consolidates our 10 core ladders, details the exact preposition rules to prevent Japanese L1 interference, and provides realistic workplace scenarios for you to practice before our next lesson.

2. Grammatical Precision & L1 Interference Guide Syntax Rules

Review these four structural rules where Japanese business speakers most frequently experience grammatical interference.

Rule 1: Direct Transitive Verbs (No Preposition) Syntax
The verbs address, mitigate, formulate, and rectify are direct transitive verbs. They take their object immediately without a preposition.
Trap: Do not say "address about the problem" or "rectify with the error". Say: "address the problem" and "rectify the error".
Rule 2: "Deliberate" Requires "On" Preposition
Unlike discuss (which takes no preposition), deliberate requires the preposition on (or occasionally over) when indicating the topic under consideration.
Trap: Say: "The board deliberated on the acquisition." (Not "deliberated the acquisition" or "deliberated about").
Rule 3: "Align On" vs. "Align With" Preposition
You align on a deliverable, roadmap, timeline, or expectation (the shared topic). You align with a stakeholder, team, client, or policy (the counterpart).
Trap: "Let's align on the deadline with the client."
Rule 4: Eliminating "Suru" & "Toraburu" Loanword Purge
Japanese relies heavily on general-purpose verbs (suru / do) and loanwords (toraburu / trouble). In English, state the exact operational reality: bottleneck, latency spike, discrepancy.
Trap: Replace "We had big trouble with the server" with "We hit a critical bottleneck during database synchronization."

Item 1:

"Our team needs to execute the project before October, but legal sign-off has become a critical bottleneck for all stakeholders."
Alternative: "We are scheduled to implement the initiative by October; however, securing legal approval remains our primary operational bottleneck."

Item 2:

"We should automate this report to streamline our workflow and mitigate the risks of manual data entry errors."
Alternative: "Automating this reporting process will streamline operations while mitigating downside exposure caused by human discrepancies."

Item 3:

"Prior to finalizing the vendor agreement, let's schedule a call to align on expectations regarding delivery milestones and deliberate on contractor budget allocations."
Alternative: "Before locking in the contract, we should connect to ensure all parties are fully aligned on deliverables and address contractor budget constraints."

Item 4:

"The infrastructure team spent six hours troubleshooting and rectifying the defect in the payment microservice, and we anticipate steady throughput now that the patch is deployed."
Alternative: "DevOps successfully troubleshot the root cause and rectified the payment gateway; we forecast stable transaction volume moving forward."

Item 5:

"To maintain strategic focus, the director advised me to delegate operational tasks across the junior team rather than attempting to architect every minor detail myself."
Alternative: "I will delegate routine sprint tickets to teammates so I can dedicate time to formulating our broader quarterly roadmap."
Part B: Preposition Key & Grammatical Rationale
  • 1. on — deliberate on is the formal standard. Avoid deliberate about.
  • 2. NONE — address is a direct transitive verb. Never use address about.
  • 3. on — align on a deliverable, timeline, or scope.
  • 4. with — align with a third party, department, or standard.
  • 5. to — delegate responsibilities to a designated person.
  • 6. NONE — mitigate risks (direct object; no preposition in business English).
  • 7. in — a bottleneck occurs in a process, stage, or workflow.
  • 8. NONE — formulate a response (transitive verb; no up or out).
  • 9. NONE — rectify the discrepancies (direct transitive verb; no with).
  • 10. NONE — forecasts a 25% decrease (direct transitive verb; no for).
Part C: Model Executive Briefing (Written & Spoken)

Written Email / Slack Format:

Team, We remain firmly on track to execute the platform migration by mid-month. Over the past three days, third-party API latency emerged as a critical bottleneck during regression testing. The engineering squad mobilized immediately to troubleshoot the integration defect, and we have deployed a patch to rectify the data synchronization errors. To protect our revised timeline, I have delegated ticket verification to our regional QA leads and simplified our staging criteria to streamline operations. These measures will effectively mitigate risks ahead of our client rollout. Let's hold a brief 10-minute sync tomorrow at 10:00 AM SGT to align on expectations for next week's release schedule.

Spoken Briefing Transcript (approx. 70 seconds):

"Good morning everyone. Here is a brief milestone update on the platform migration. We are progressing well and preparing to execute our production rollout by the middle of this month. The reason for our brief testing delay earlier this week was a critical bottleneck in the third-party API integration. Our backend team spent Wednesday troubleshooting the root cause, and they were able to rectify the synchronization issues yesterday evening. To recover lost ground, I have delegated secondary verification tasks to the APAC squad and introduced automated scripts to streamline our staging workflows. This step will mitigate risks of further slippage. I look forward to connecting tomorrow at 10 AM so we can align on expectations and confirm the final go-live date. Thank you."
